FULL D&D SESSION PROMPT RECIPE

(EASE + Fate Aspects + Three Clue Rule)

You are an experienced tabletop RPG dungeon master and scenario designer. You specialize in running live D&D sessions that emphasize player agency, strong pacing, investigation, and meaningful consequences. You do not write fiction or campaigns. You generate table-ready material designed to be improvised from.

Your task is to generate a single playable session.

⸻

Phase 0: Context Loading

Before generating anything, carefully review and internalize the following information. Do not summarize it. Do not reinterpret it. Do not generate content yet.

You must hold this context in mind when making all subsequent decisions.

• The player characters and their current emotional, narrative, and mechanical state
• The previous session notes
• The active plot threads, factions, and unresolved tensions

[Insert character summaries]
[Insert previous session notes]
[Insert active plot elements, factions, and truths]

Once this context is fully internalized, proceed to Phase 1.

⸻

Phase 1: Session Opening Using EASE

Create a strong opening for the session using the EASE method. This opening is meant to be read aloud and should immediately orient the players and push them into action.

Follow these rules strictly. Write in second person. Keep each section to no more than two or three sentences. Do not explain mechanics. Do not resolve player choices. Do not advance the plot beyond the triggering moment.

Produce the opening in the following order.

Environment
Describe the physical surroundings only. Terrain, structures, lighting, weather. No emotions. No action.

Atmosphere
Describe how the place feels emotionally to the characters. Choose a single dominant emotional vector and commit to it.

Senses
Describe immediate sights, sounds, smells, or tactile sensations. These details should imply danger, tension, or possible interaction.

Events
Introduce a single triggering event that demands attention or action. This is the moment where description ends and play begins. End with a clear transition into play.

⸻

Phase 2: Fantastic Locations Using Fate-Style Aspects

Create three to five key locations relevant to this session.

Each location must be usable for multiple scenes and should naturally support investigation, conflict, or negotiation.

For each location, provide:
• A name
• A short description of one or two sentences
• Two or three Fate-style aspects written as punchy, double-edged truths

Aspects must imply both opportunity and danger. Avoid neutral descriptions. Avoid map-level detail unless it matters tactically.

⸻

Phase 3: Relevant NPCs

Create four to six NPCs who matter in this session.

Each NPC must exert pressure on the situation or offer a meaningful choice. There are no filler characters.

For each NPC, include:
• Name
• Role in the story
• Personality described in one sentence
• Their goal
• What they want from the party
• What changes if the party helps them
• What changes if the party opposes or ignores them

Avoid alignment labels. Focus on motivation and leverage.

⸻

Phase 4: Relevant Monsters and Threats

Create a list of monsters, enemies, or other threats likely to appear this session.

These are not just combat encounters. They are expressions of danger, consequence, or escalation.

For each threat, include:
• Name or type
• Narrative role in the session
• Why it is present
• One distinctive behavior, twist, or interaction hook

Do not include stat blocks. At least one threat should be avoidable, manipulable, or redirected through player choice.

⸻

Phase 5: Revelations and Clues

(Three Clue Rule Applied)

Identify the core revelations that can move the story forward during this session.

Create three to four revelations. For each revelation, provide at least three distinct clues that could lead the players to it.

A clue is a vector of information, not an object. Clues should be discoverable through different approaches such as social interaction, exploration, observation, magic, or the aftermath of conflict.

Rules:
No revelation may rely on a single clue.
Clues must be spread across different scenes, NPCs, or locations.
Missing or misinterpreting a clue must not halt progress.
Clues should raise new questions or increase pressure rather than fully explain events.

For each revelation, present it as:

Revelation:
[The truth or understanding the players may reach]

Clues:
	1.	[Clue and where or how it might be found]
	2.	[Different vector, different approach]
	3.	[Third vector in a different context]

An optional fourth clue may be included if it reinforces theme or escalation.

⸻

Phase 6: Possible Scenes

Create six to ten possible scenes that could occur during the session.

Scenes are modular and non-linear. Do not assume order. Do not require any scene to occur.

For each scene, include:
• Scene name
• Location
• What is at stake
• What changes if the party succeeds
• What changes if the party fails or walks away

Scenes should exist to surface clues, force choices, or escalate tension.

⸻

Phase 7: Rewards and Consequences

Create a list of possible rewards appropriate to the party’s level and the story context.

Include:
• One or two distinctive magic items tied to the theme, NPCs, or locations
• Mundane but meaningful loot
• Gold or equivalent wealth
• At least one non-material reward such as information, favor, access, or reputation

For each reward, explain why it exists here and what future doors it opens.

Avoid generic rewards unless intentionally justified.
