# The Kitsune Village Awakening

**When to use:** Immediately after Thornbringer waters the One Tree and the sage vanishes. The party turns away from the tree.

**Tone target:** The sage scene was held breath and stone silence. This is the exhale. The contrast should hit like stepping through a doorway — same cavern, different world. Move from wonder to warmth to welcome across the three blocks below.

---

## Block 1: The Turning

*Read this the moment the party turns away from the tree.*

> You turn.
>
> The cavern behind you is not what it was.
>
> The darkness that filled this place — that heavy, waiting emptiness you walked through to reach the tree — is gone. In its place: light. Paper lanterns, dozens of them, strung on lines you did not see before, swaying gently from posts and eaves that were not there a moment ago. Their glow is amber and rose and the pale gold of early evening, and it falls across rooftops — low buildings of dark timber and pale plaster, tightly packed, lining a lane that curves away into the cavern's deeper dark. Prayer strips flutter from every lintel. Wind chimes made of bone and river-glass turn in a breeze that has no source.
>
> The smell reaches you before anything else. Rice wine, warm and sweet. Charcoal smoke and something fatty and rich — meat, maybe, or fish, or something that has no name in the upper city. The smell of a kitchen working hard, of a hearth fed all day, of a place that has been feeding people for a very long time and is very glad to be doing it again.
>
> Then: sound. Laughter, high and cascading, from somewhere around that curve. The sharp knock of a cup set down on wood. A voice — two voices — a short burst of argument followed immediately by more laughter.
>
> This place was ruins. You walked through it. It was empty and cold and silent. And now it is none of those things.

**[Pause here. Let players react. Field observations and questions with "yes" — the lanterns are real, the smells are real, the sounds are real. If they try to touch something, it is solid and warm. If they look for where the edge of the new village meets the old cavern, the transition is seamless, as if it was always there and they simply could not see it.]**

---

## Block 2: The Village Lane

*Read this when the party moves toward the light.*

> The lane opens around you as you step forward. Buildings on both sides now, close enough that you could touch the walls with outstretched hands. Window screens glow from within. Through one, you see the silhouette of someone moving with quick, certain steps — setting tables, maybe, or carrying trays. Through another, smaller shapes chase each other across the floor.
>
> And then you see them.
>
> The first is sitting on a low step outside a dyer's shop, cleaning her face with the back of her wrist the way a cat would. She is mostly human in shape — a woman in a rust-colored robe, her hair pinned up with lacquered sticks — except for the ears, which are fox ears, orange-tipped and swiveled toward you with sharp attention. And the tail, two of them, curling behind her in slow, independent arcs. She freezes when she sees you. Her ears flatten. Then she grins.
>
> A fox bolts across the lane in front of you — small, red, three tails spinning behind it like a comet. It slides under a barrel and peers out, nose working.
>
> Above you, on a rooftop, a child sits watching — fox-eared, bright-eyed, legs dangling over the edge. When your eyes find them, they vanish. Not run away. Vanish. A giggle drifts down from thin air.
>
> At the far end of the lane, past the press of buildings and the drifting lantern-smoke, a larger structure anchors the space. Two stories. A curtained doorway with a banner above it — the kanji for spirit, and welcome, and something older that you cannot quite parse. Warm light spills through every seam in its walls. The sound of voices inside is a low roar, like a river heard from a distance.
>
> The sake house.

**[Another pause. This is the moment players will start talking to each other, pointing things out, asking what to do. Let them. The kitsune woman on the step is watching with open amusement. The one under the barrel is still there. The rooftop child is still giggling from wherever they went. None of them approach yet — they are watching, assessing, entertained.]**

---

## Block 3: Noticed

*Read this when the party makes a clear decision to approach, or after roughly a minute of deliberation — whichever comes first.*

> Word moves through the village faster than you do.
>
> You cannot see it happen, but you can feel it — a ripple of attention, like the moment a school of fish all turns at once. Faces appear in windows. A head pokes around a doorframe, then ducks back. The three-tailed fox under the barrel pads out into the lane, sits down, and stares at you with gold eyes.
>
> The woman on the step stands. She is taller than you expected. Her two tails fan slowly behind her, and she tilts her head — curious, not afraid — with the particular patience of a creature that has seen many centuries and finds most of them interesting.
>
> "You watered the tree." Her voice is unhurried, warm, accented in a way that suggests she learned your language a very long time ago from people who have been dead for generations. It is not a question. "We felt it. The whole village felt it, like—" She pauses, turns her wrist over, looking for the word. "Like when a bell stops ringing and you realize how much you had stopped hearing it."
>
> She glances back at the sake house. Then at you. Then, quite deliberately, back at the sake house.
>
> "The house has been waiting a long time to open its doors." A pause. "It would consider it rude if you walked past."
>
> From somewhere inside, a new burst of laughter, and the high ringing note of several cups raised at once.

**[The woman's name is Misaki — use it if asked, or have another kitsune call her by name. She is not in charge, exactly, but she is the first to speak because she is the most curious and the least worried. She will answer questions, but kitsune answers are often interesting before they are useful. If players ask what this place is: "It has always been here. You simply were not yet the kind of people who could see it." If they ask about the Oni Mage or the prince: she sobers immediately. She knows something. She will not say it in the lane — that conversation belongs inside, with drinks, like serious things should be.)]**

## Running Notes

### What the Village Is

This is a kitsune settlement that exists in the boundary between the physical cavern and the spirit world. It was not created tonight — it has always been coexistent with the One Tree. When the tree was diminished, the village fell dormant: present but invisible, silent, inaccessible. Thornbringer's act of restoration did not build this place. It woke it up.

The kitsune here range from one-tail (young, mischievous, fox-form or near-fox) to five or six tails (elder, more humanoid, deeply magical). Misaki has two tails, which makes her young by their reckoning but old by any human measure.

### What the Sake House Offers

The sake house itself, the Amber Threshold (or let players name it if they ask), functions like a spirit-world waystation. The drinks are real. The food is real. The company is real. It is also a place where information moves differently — kitsune trade in stories and secrets the way humans trade in coin. A player who sits down and shares something true, something that cost them something to say, will get something useful back.

Do not plan what they learn inside. Let it emerge from what the party talks about and who is listening.

### Misaki as NPC

**Voice:** Warm, measured, slightly amused by everything. Never hurried. Speaks like someone who has time because she has had so much of it.

**Motivation:** Curious about the party, genuinely grateful the tree is restored, but not naive about what woke it up or what is happening above. She knows the upper city is in trouble. She is deciding how much she wants to be involved.

**Tell:** When she is about to say something she considers important, her tails go still.

**One line to open with if conversation stalls:** "You carry a great deal of weight for people who just did something kind. That is unusual. Usually people who do kind things do them lightly."

### Kitsune Table (Quick Reference for Improvisation)

Roll or choose to populate the sake house and village with distinct individuals.

### The Morning Exit

The village does not trap anyone. When the party is ready to leave, the lane leads back the way they came — but the cavern will be warm now, and the lanterns will remain lit, a soft glow visible from the base of the One Tree. The kitsune are not a resource to be spent. They are an ongoing relationship, available to players who treat them as such.

If anyone tries to mark the location on a map, the ink dries in the wrong place. The village finds its own.
