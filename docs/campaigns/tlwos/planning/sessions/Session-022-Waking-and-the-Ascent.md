# Session 022: Waking in the Cold — Ascent to the Upper City

> Canon status (2026-04-08): This prep document has been played.
> Canon recap: [Session 022: Beneath the Brewery of Ninefold Reflections](../../../../posts/tlw-022-session.md)
> Current campaign state: [TLWoS Current State](../../current-state.md)

**Scene arc:** Emotional landing after the underworld sequence — grief of the vanished village, warmth confirmed real, then ascent into a city that has become a shadow version of itself. Ronin Warriors dark-world energy: reality warped, architecture grown wrong, citizens aligned into a collective hum, shadows flowing toward the palace like dark water. The Consent Gate encounter tests everything the party learned, now against the backdrop of a city that has already been largely consumed.

**Tone target:** Begin in quiet grief, move through mounting wrongness as the city degrades around them, arrive at the encounter in visceral dread — not because the threat is monstrous, but because the monstrous thing has already happened and the city is still smiling.

---

## Part One: The Village Is Gone

### Read-Aloud: Waking

*Read this before any player speaks. Let the silence do work.*

> You wake to cold.
>
> Not the cold of a bad night's sleep, not the cold of morning before a fire is lit. The cold of a space that has not held warmth in a very long time. Stone on your back. The smell of dust and old water. The smell of nothing.
>
> You open your eyes.
>
> The cavern is dark. Not dim — dark. The lanterns are gone. The lane is gone. The buildings with their warm-lit screens and their prayer strips and their wind chimes made of bone and river-glass — gone. What you slept in was this: a scatter of broken foundations, low walls worn to ankle height by decades of water and settling, the remnants of something that may once have been a structure and is now simply rubble in an arrangement that suggests the ghost of intention.
>
> The cavern is silent the way it was when you first walked through it. Heavy. Patient. Waiting.
>
> You are lying in the ruins. It is cold and it is dark and there is no sake house and there was never a lane and the lanterns never happened and —
>
> Your chest is warm.
>
> Exactly there, behind the sternum. Banked, like a fire built for the night.

**[Pause. Hold this. Do not rush to reassure players. Let them sit in the confusion for a moment. If they ask questions, answer with sensory specifics — the warmth is real, they can feel it. Then point them toward the evidence.]**

---

### Read-Aloud: What Remains

*Read this in response to players reaching for proof — or add it as a second beat if they go quiet.*

> The One Tree still glows. Quiet and steady, the same color it was last night, the same color it has been since Thornbringer cupped his hands and carried water from a place underneath the world. Green-white. Alive.
>
> And the gifts are real.
>
> Whatever was placed in your hands last night — whatever was offered at a table in a room full of warm noise — you still have it. The warmth in your chest is still there. The knowledge is still there. The things that were said to you in a space that no longer appears to exist — those are still there too.
>
> In the dust beside where you slept, there is a faint impression. The shape of a cushion, pressed into old silt. Just one, for each of you. As if something very soft sat here a long time and left a mark.

**[DM note: Thornbringer's eyes still burn green. Fox still has the paper strip if it was earned. Yasei can perceive, if they reach for it, a faint spiritual resonance in the cavern — not active, not present, but a kind of warmth in the stone, like a handprint left on glass. The kitsune were here. They are still here, in the way that things that have decided to be somewhere are always there, even when they cannot be seen. The village did not vanish. It went back to sleep. The lanterns are dark. They are not gone.]**

---

### DM Direction: Managing the Emotional Beat

This scene lands differently for different players. Some will process through action. Some will want to talk to each other. Some will go quiet.

Do not skip this scene to get to the encounter. The encounter only works if the party has spent a moment with what they carry.

If the table needs a prompt: Misaki said "bring the truth of whatever happened — not the version you tell other people." Ask the player of Fox, or whoever is most likely to have something to say, what their character does first. Not what they do next. First.

If Thornbringer reaches out to touch the cavern floor or a nearby root: "The pulse is there. Faint. The same one you felt in the tree last night. The earth has noted you."

If Yasei listens: "The resonance in this place has a temperature to it now. Warm. Like a room just after the people in it have left."

If Aoshi says nothing and simply picks up his gear: that is correct. Let him.

## Part Two: The Ascent

### Read-Aloud: Moving Upward — The City Changes

*Read as the party begins climbing toward the city levels. The wrongness should build gradually — not a sudden shift, but a slow realization that the world is not the one they left.*

> The path back up through Takashima is not the one you came down.
>
> At first it is small things. The stairs cut from the living rock are the same stairs, but the stone is darker than you remember — not dirty, not stained. Darker, as if the color has been drawn out of it from underneath. The maintenance tunnels feel narrower. The ceiling lower. You did not duck on the way down. You are ducking now.
>
> The first wrong thing you see clearly: a lantern mounted on a wall bracket at the tunnel's bend. It is lit. The flame inside it is the wrong color — not orange, not even the blue-white of alchemical fire. It is a pale, flat grey, the color of a sky that has forgotten whether it is dawn or dusk, and it gives light without warmth. Your shadow falls behind you in a direction that does not match where the flame is.
>
> Then you hear the city.
>
> Not the siege's aftermath — that was noise, human noise, the sounds of cleaning up and counting the cost. This is different. The city above you is humming. A low, sustained vibration in the stone, felt through the soles of your feet before it reaches your ears. It is the sound a city makes when it is dreaming, and the dream is not its own.

**[Pause. Let them process. If they try to identify the sound: it is not mechanical, not magical in any way they can classify. It is structural. The city itself is resonating at a frequency that was not there yesterday.]**

---

### Read-Aloud: The Lower City — Shadow Register

*Read as they emerge from the tunnels into the lower city.*

> You step out of the tunnels and the sky is wrong.
>
> There is no sun. There should be — it is morning, you know it is morning, you can feel the hour in your bones the way anyone who has slept and woken can feel it. But the sky above Takashima is a flat, featureless grey. Not overcast. Not clouded. Grey, the way a ceiling is grey — close, uniform, giving nothing. The light that reaches the streets has no source and no direction. It casts no shadows, or rather, it casts shadows that do not correspond to anything above them.
>
> The lower city is moving. People are in the streets — merchants, laborers, a woman carrying water, children near a doorway. The ward markets have reopened. Transactions are happening. But the rhythm is wrong. Too even. Too synchronized. You watch a merchant hand a bundle to a customer and the customer turns away and the merchant reaches for the next bundle and the customer at the next stall turns away at the same time, the same angle, the same measured step.
>
> No one is talking.
>
> They are moving their mouths. You can see lips form words, jaws working, the small nods that mean agreement. But the sound that reaches you is not language. It is the hum — the same hum from the tunnels, carried on a hundred moving mouths, resonating off the stone walls like the whole district is a struck bell.
>
> A guard at a lower checkpoint stands in full dress. Clean armor — twelve hours after a siege. His eyes track you as you pass. He does not speak. He smiles. The smile is correct. Every part of the smile is correct. It does not reach anything behind his eyes because there is nothing behind his eyes that is currently his.

**[DM note: The lower city is operating in a shadow register — reality has been overlaid. The people are real. They are not possessed in the traditional sense. They are *aligned* — their rhythms have been synchronized to the Oni's sustained tone. Think of it as a city sleepwalking in formation. They will respond to direct address, but their answers are slightly too helpful, slightly too organized, and they all pause at the same points in their sentences. If the party asks a direct question, the nearest person answers with precision and courtesy. If the party asks a second person the same question, they get the same answer — same words, same cadence, same pauses. The information is accurate. The people delivering it are not entirely present.]**

**[If Yasei reaches out with spiritual sensitivity: the people are dimmed. Not gone — dimmed, like a lantern turned down to its lowest setting. Their spiritual signatures are present but muted, subsumed into the larger resonance. The hum is not coming from them. It is coming through them.]**

**[If Thornbringer reaches for the natural world — touches wood, stone, earth: the pulse from the One Tree is still there, but faint. Fainter than it was in the cavern. Something is dampening it. The higher they climb, the fainter it will get.]**

---

### Read-Aloud: The Middle Terrace — The Smell

> The middle terrace is worse.
>
> Not louder, not more dramatic — worse in the way that something you almost got used to suddenly reveals a deeper layer. The buildings here are the same buildings but their colors have bled. The red of a dyer's banner is the red of old blood, dried to brown at its edges. The white plaster walls have a faint grey marbling, like veins visible beneath skin. The timber frames are darkened past weathering into something closer to char, though nothing has burned.
>
> The smell reaches you here. Not charcoal and recovery. Not cooking fires and people. Cedar smoke — but wrong. The cedar smell of a shrine turned inside out, sacred wood burned for a purpose that has nothing to do with prayer. Underneath it, something older and sweeter, the way flowers smell when they are past dying and have entered the stage of returning to the earth. The smell of something being composted that is not yet finished being alive.
>
> And the shadows. You notice them now because they have started to move independently. Not dramatically — not leaping or crawling. Just slightly out of sync with the bodies casting them. A woman walks past a wall and her shadow arrives at the corner a half-step late. A child's shadow stretches longer than the grey light should allow, reaching toward the upper terrace like a hand extended toward something it wants.
>
> Every shadow in the middle terrace points upward.

**[DM note: The shadow behavior is the key visual tell. Every shadow in the city — cast by people, buildings, objects — leans toward the palace in the upper city, regardless of light source. Players who notice this and follow the direction are being shown the geometry of the Oni's influence: everything flows upward, toward the center. The city is not being controlled from above. It is being drawn toward above, the way water is drawn toward a drain.]**

## Part Three: The Consent Gate

### Overview

**What this is:** The primary checkpoint between the middle and upper terraces of Takashima. Under ordinary circumstances, a bureaucratic formality — district passes, guard acknowledgment, nominal inspection. Today it is something else.

**What has happened:** The senior aide mentioned below (a mid-tier Oni servant, not the Mage himself) has spent the night restructuring this checkpoint. The guards on duty have not been corrupted in any dramatic sense. They have simply been very well organized, very clearly briefed, and are operating under a standing authorization to offer "temporary upper-city access passes" to individuals who agree to specific terms.

The terms are entirely reasonable. That is the point.

**The threat:** Not a locked gate. Not hostile guards. A series of reasonable offers that, taken together, constitute a binding agreement — and the party has a paper strip that reveals the true cost of any agreement. The encounter is about whether they use it, when, and what they do with what they see.

---

### Read-Aloud: Approaching the Gate

*Read as the party arrives at the upper terrace checkpoint.*

> The gate between the middle and upper terraces has changed.
>
> You remember stone pillars and a timber crossbar — functional, unremarkable. What you see now is this: the pillars are taller than they were. Not rebuilt. Taller, as if the stone has grown upward overnight, tapering to points that disappear into the grey ceiling of sky. The timber crossbar is black — not painted, not charred. Black the way something is black when light has decided not to touch it. Characters have been carved into the crossbar that you do not remember being there. They are not in any language you read. They move when you are not looking directly at them.
>
> There are six guards. Their armor is the same armor that was on these same bodies twelve hours ago during a siege. It is clean now, polished to a mirror finish, and in the flat grey light the reflections in the metal do not show the street behind you. They show something else — angles, corridors, a geometry that does not match Takashima's architecture. The guards stand perfectly still. Not at attention. Still, the way objects are still. None of them blink at the same time, but they blink in sequence — left to right, left to right — like a wave passing through them.
>
> Then one steps forward. He is perhaps forty, broad through the shoulders, a district-commander's sigil at his collar. And he is — different. His eyes have something behind them. Not the dimmed quality of the people below. Something still present, still struggling. He is sweating at the temples despite the cold. His hands grip the ledger like it is the last thing in this city that makes sense to him.
>
> "Good morning," he says. His voice catches on the second word, just slightly, as if he had to remember how the sound is supposed to work. Then it steadies, and what comes out is professional, measured, and almost — almost — normal. "The upper terrace is under a provisional access protocol while restoration efforts continue. This is a routine measure." A pause. Something flickers across his face — confusion, maybe, or the ghost of a question he cannot quite form. Then it is gone. "I have been asked to facilitate passage for a small number of individuals conducting sensitive business in the upper wards. You may be those individuals. May I ask the nature of your business and the name of your sponsoring contact?"

**[DM note: His name is Takeo Ren. He is the most important person in this scene because he is the only one at this gate who is still partially himself. The other five guards are fully aligned — present but emptied, operating on the Oni's rhythm. Takeo is resisting. He does not know he is resisting. He thinks he is doing his job and having a bad morning. The sweat, the catch in his voice, the flicker of confusion — these are a man's identity pushing back against something that is trying to smooth him into the pattern. He was briefed by someone from the upper city with all the right paperwork. He followed the instructions because they were reasonable. But some part of him has not yet agreed, and that part is what the party can reach.]**

**[If Yasei reads him spiritually: Takeo's signature is flickering. Not dimmed like the others — flickering, like a flame in a draft. Something is trying to settle over him and it has not fully taken hold. He has maybe hours, not days.]**

---

### The Offer — Three Layers

**Takeo will offer the following, in order, based on what the party says. Each offer is legitimate. Each offer costs something. The party does not know what it costs until they read the paper strip.**

#### Layer One: Verified Passage

> "If you can provide a current district pass or a letter of introduction from a recognized upper-city contact, passage is immediate. Standard terms — you are logged by name and entry time, you agree to present to the watch if asked during your stay, and you agree not to access restricted archive or administrative sections without escort."

**What this costs (visible on the paper strip):** The names and stated business are transmitted — via courier, within the hour — to a court-sealed routing office. The routing office is operated by the senior aide. The information will arrive in the upper city before the party does.

**Readable on the strip:** *The truth of this offer: your names and purpose are already upstairs before you climb the first step.*

---

#### Layer Two: Provisional Pass (No Contact Required)

> "Alternatively — and this is a new provision, implemented this morning — individuals with demonstrable civic interest in upper-city stability may apply for a provisional pass. You would agree to check in with a watch station within three hours of entering the upper terrace, state your purpose to the supervising sergeant, and agree that your activities during that window are considered to be in service of district restoration. There is no fee. The pass is good for one day." He looks at you with no expression that isn't professional courtesy. "This is the mechanism being used to facilitate the reconstruction coordination. It is standard."

**What this costs (visible on the paper strip):** The "in service of district restoration" clause is a legal binding. Anyone who takes this pass is, for twenty-four hours, operating in an official capacity — which means their actions are logged, attributable, and — in a city under provisional authority — subject to review by whoever holds restoration authority. The Oni's network holds restoration authority. This pass makes the party agents of the same office that is managing the prince.

**Readable on the strip:** *The truth of this offer: for one day, you work for them. Every door you open, every word you say, in their name.*

---

#### Layer Three: The Informal Favor

*This only comes if the party tries to deflect or appear to give up. Takeo lowers his voice slightly — not conspiratorially, but in the register of a man who is trying to be helpful.*

> "I understand if neither option suits you. There is a third path, though it is — less formal." He glances at the ledger, then back at the party. "A number of individuals conducting sensitive operations have been granted informal access at the discretion of the supervising commander — myself, currently — in exchange for a simple agreement to share any information gathered in the upper city that bears on district security. This is not reporting. It is a courtesy exchange. One conversation with a watch commander upon departure." He pauses. "I am authorized to make this offer. It is entirely at your discretion."

**What this costs (visible on the paper strip):** The "courtesy exchange" is not optional once agreed to. It is a binding debrief obligation under temporary emergency powers — the party must disclose what they found. What they disclose goes to the routing office. The Oni will know what they know, within hours of them knowing it.

**Readable on the strip:** *The truth of this offer: this is the door that locks behind you. You go in free. You come out empty.*

---

### Decision Points

**The paper strip is the hinge of this encounter.** The party has it. Fox has it, specifically. The question is: when do they use it, and what do they do with what they see?

**If they use the strip on the first offer:** They see it immediately and have maximum information with maximum time to decide. They also have to play it cool — Takeo did not do anything wrong. He does not know what his ledger transmits. Accusing him achieves nothing and gains an enemy.

**If they use the strip on the third offer:** The horror of it lands harder, because the first two felt reasonable, and now they can see that all three of them were the same structure, just wearing different clothes.

**If they never use the strip:** They may agree to one of the terms without knowing the cost. This is not a failure state. Let the consequences play out — at some point in the upper city, someone will know something they shouldn't, and the party will understand why.

---

### How Takeo Responds to Various Approaches

**If the party invokes a legitimate contact (Kaoru Sugimoto, the Hidden Lotus network, or anyone from the lower city):**
Takeo listens, writes it down, and says the contact will be verified and they may wait. The wait is real — twenty minutes minimum. During the wait, the party can observe the checkpoint, ask Takeo questions, or attempt other solutions. The verification goes upward and the answer that comes back is yes — but that answer also carries their association with Kaoru to the routing office.

**If the party tries to bluff their way through:**
Takeo is not easily bluffed, but he is not hostile. He will ask clarifying questions with the patience of someone who has time. He does not raise the alarm unless someone becomes threatening. A good deception (DC 15) gets them through with the third offer applied automatically — the very act of smooth entry was the agreement. The strip, if used after the fact, shows: *The truth of this passage: you agreed without being asked.*

**If the party tries to find another way up:**
There are two. One is a maintenance access route through the waterworks infrastructure — but it has been sealed this morning. The seals are fresh. One is a shrine path that requires a monastic token or an active spiritual sponsor — Yasei may be able to invoke the One Tree's resonance here (DC 14 Religion or Spiritual Insight, advantage if they mention the kitsune or the tree). The shrine path is unwatched and unlogged.

**If the party calls out the structure directly — tells Takeo they know what these offers are:**
The flickering in him surges. For a moment his eyes clear — fully clear, the first fully human eyes the party has seen since they left the cavern — and he looks at them like a drowning man who just felt a hand. If they are specific enough, he goes still. Not guilty. Waking. DC 13 Persuasion (lower if they show the paper strip, lower still if they say something genuinely true about what is happening to this city). He lets them through off-ledger. His hands shake as he does it. He may whisper: "Something is wrong with me. I have been — I can't remember agreeing to —" He cannot finish the sentence. The hum reasserts. His eyes dim slightly. But not all the way. Not yet. He has hours. The party can come back for him, or they cannot, and that is a choice that will cost them something either way.

**If the party tries to physically help Takeo (remove the influence):**
They cannot. Not here, not now. The alignment is not a spell to be dispelled — it is a frequency to be disrupted, and the source is in the palace. Yasei will know this instinctively. Thornbringer may feel the One Tree's pulse push back weakly against the hum when he touches Takeo, buying the man a moment of clarity but nothing lasting. The cure is upstream.

### The Shrine Path Resolution

*If the party finds or takes the shrine path, read this.*

> The shrine path should not still exist.
>
> Everything around it has changed — the walls darkened, the shadows pouring, the geometry of the city pulled into the Oni's design. But the pilgrim's route is still here, shallow steps worn by centuries of devotional feet, winding up through a gap in the terrace wall between a shrine to a fortune deity and a public rest house with its blinds down. The gap is narrow. The shadow-rivers flow around it on both sides, like water parting around a stone, and do not enter.
>
> The rope barrier is old. The prayer strip hanging from it is yellowed and soft. The characters say, in a script older than the city above: *Those who are here for the city's sake — pass freely.*
>
> The grey light does not reach this path. Instead, as you step past the rope, there is — for exactly three steps — warmth. Not heat. The specific warmth of the sake house. The banked-fire warmth behind the sternum. The color of the stone under your feet shifts, just faintly, toward green. Then it fades, and you are on the upper terrace, and the dark world closes around you again.
>
> But for three steps, the city remembered what it was.

**[DM note: The shrine path is a scar that the Oni's influence could not close — the oldest spiritual infrastructure in Takashima, predating the palace, the terraces, the city itself. It is connected to the One Tree through root-channels in the bedrock. The Oni's alignment cannot reach it for the same reason the kitsune village could wake: the tree's network is a different frequency entirely, and it does not harmonize. Yasei should feel this as a sharp clarity — stepping onto the path is like surfacing from underwater. Thornbringer's pulse SURGES here, loud and clear for three steps, then dims again as they enter the upper city. Those three steps are a promise: the counter-frequency exists. It just needs to be louder.]**

### DM Notes: Running the Encounter

**The encounter's job:** To make the party feel how the Oni works before they meet him. The gate itself is not evil. The guards are not villains. The offers are locally legitimate. The trap is in the structure — in the way each reasonable thing connects to the next reasonable thing until the path has been built. The party now understands, in their bodies, what "the first lie is never forced, it is agreed to" means.

**Do not tell them what the strip shows all at once.** Let each offer arrive separately. Let the reveal unfold at the party's pace. If Fox reads the strip privately (whispering to one other character), let that be a moment of held breath.

**Takeo should feel like a person.** He is not a minion. He is a competent, decent man who was given an efficient briefing and asked to implement it. If the party ends this scene having made him an ally, or even just having made him uncertain — that is a win. He may be useful later. He may also be at risk.

**The encounter has no combat state.** If the party draws weapons or threatens the guards, Takeo will lock down the checkpoint and send for reinforcements. Nothing in this encounter requires or rewards aggression. The only ways through are: agree (and carry the cost), find the crack (shrine path), or change the structure itself (turn Takeo). All three are valid. All three teach the party something about what they are walking into.

**Pacing:** This encounter should take 20-35 minutes. If it goes longer, it is because the party is talking — which is good. Keep Takeo engaged and responsive. Do not let him become a wall. He is trying to help. The horror is that he means it.

### What the Party Carries Into the Upper City

Regardless of how they get through, give each player a moment to name one thing their character knows or carries that the Oni's network does not. This is the inventory of advantage going into the political arc.

The kitsune gave them:
- The prince has not slept in a month
- He wakes at the same hour and remembers nothing
- The agreements together clear a path for something specific
- The Oni works through reflection — showing people the version of themselves they wish were true
- The Oni cannot maintain this in the presence of genuine grief
- A man with a code and a man who performs one look identical until they don't — this is the advantage
- Something in the upper city is singing in a register it should not be able to reach

The ascent showed them:
- The city has been physically transformed — architecture warped, shadows flowing toward the palace, citizens aligned into a collective hum
- The alignment is a frequency, not a spell — it cannot be dispelled, only disrupted at the source
- Takeo Ren is still partially himself — the alignment is not instant or absolute, resistance is possible
- The shrine path exists as a scar the Oni cannot close — the One Tree's frequency is the counter-note
- The transformation deepens in layers: lower city (dimmed), middle terrace (bleeding), upper city (fully realized shadow world)

They have a paper strip that reads true cost. They have Thornbringer's green flame eyes and the One Tree's pulse as a compass — stronger toward truth, dimmer toward alignment. They have Fox's blank strip if it was earned. They have one night of having said real things in a place that remembered them for it. They have three steps of warmth on a shrine path that proved the counter-frequency exists.

The Oni does not know any of this. He hears the song. He tracks the names. He manages the geometry.

He does not know that something in the roots is already singing back.

### Hookline Into the Next Scene

*Read this as they clear the upper terrace threshold — whichever way they came through.*

> The upper city is a wound wearing a crown.
>
> You remember it as beautiful — lacquered buildings, wide streets, rooflines curving upward like hands raised in invocation. The bones of that beauty are still here. But they have been hollowed out and filled with something else, the way a hermit crab fills a shell it did not grow.
>
> The streets are wider than they should be. Not renovated — stretched, as if the geometry of the district has been pulled apart at the seams to make room for something that needs more space than the city was built to give. The buildings are still dark wood against pale stone, but the lacquer has deepened past black into a color that drinks the grey light and gives nothing back. The rooflines still curve upward, but they curve too far now — they do not stop where architecture stops. They taper into points that seem to continue upward past where your eyes can follow, vanishing into the flat sky like hooks sunk into the belly of the clouds.
>
> The shadows here do not lean toward the palace.
>
> They pour.
>
> Every shadow in the upper city — from the eaves, from the walls, from the bodies of the figures moving with synchronized precision through the streets — flows toward the palace like dark water running downhill. The streets are patterned with it: rivers of shadow cutting across the stone, all converging on a single point ahead of you where the palace compound rises above the surrounding rooftops.
>
> And the sound. The hum you have been hearing since the tunnels, the resonance that came through the walls and up through the soles of your feet — here it is not a hum. Here it is a voice. Not words. Not language. A sustained, open-throated tone, like something enormous exhaling in a register that the human body was not designed to perceive but perceives anyway, in the teeth, in the joints, in the place behind the eyes where headaches are born.
>
> It is coming from the palace. It has been coming from the palace for thirty days. And now that you are close enough to hear it clearly, you understand something that the distance obscured:
>
> It is not one voice.
>
> It is hundreds. Every aligned soul in Takashima, breathing in concert, feeding a single sustained note back toward the thing that taught them to sing.
>
> You are in the upper city. The sky is wrong and the shadows pour and the city is singing with a mouth that is not its own.
>
> And somewhere in the palace, something is listening to make sure the song does not stop.

**[DM note: The upper city is the Oni's domain fully realized — the shadow world in its mature state. The lower city was early-stage alignment: people dimmed, rhythms synchronized, but still functional, still recognizably human. The middle terrace was transitional: colors bleeding, shadows diverging. Here, reality itself has been restructured around the Oni's will. The architecture has physically warped. The shadows have direction. The hum has become a chorus. This is what happens when incremental consent reaches critical mass — not a sudden transformation, but a slow saturation until the original thing can no longer be distinguished from what replaced it. The prince is at the center of this. He is not the source. He is the instrument — the first voice that all the others learned to harmonize with. If the party can reach him, if they can crack the reflection, the song loses its conductor. The voices will not know what note to hold. The shadows will not know where to flow. The geometry will stutter. This is not guaranteed to work. But it is the shape of the solution.]**

**[Thornbringer's pulse from the One Tree is very faint here — almost inaudible under the hum. But it is there. It is the only thing in the upper city that is not part of the song. If he focuses on it, he can use it like a compass: the pulse grows slightly stronger when he moves toward something true, and dims when he moves toward something aligned. This is not precise. It is a feeling. But in a city where everything else has been tuned to a single frequency, one stubborn counter-note is worth everything.]**

## Quick Reference

### Takeo Ren (Guard Commander)
- **Voice:** Measured, professional, genuinely helpful. Never intimidating.
- **Tell:** Goes still when surprised. Processing, not threatening.
- **Motivation:** Doing his job well. Believes his orders are legitimate.

### The Three Offers (Summary)
| Offer | Surface | True Cost (Strip) |
|-------|---------|-------------------|
| Verified Passage | Standard log and inspection | Names and purpose transmitted upstairs in advance |
| Provisional Pass | Free one-day civic access | Legal binding: party becomes agents of the restoration authority for 24 hours |
| Informal Favor | One debrief on departure | Mandatory disclosure; everything the party found goes to the Oni's network |

### Shrine Path
- Requires: Yasei spiritual roll (DC 14) or visible pilgrim purpose
- Advantage if: party invokes the One Tree or kitsune by name
- Result: Clean entry, unlogged, untracked

### Paper Strip Trigger Points
- First offer — maximum information, maximum decision time
- Third offer — maximum horror, least time
- After entry — retroactive understanding of what they just did
