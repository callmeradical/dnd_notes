# Education and Literacy in Shuigari

## Overview

Education in Shuigari is rigidly stratified by social class, reflecting the feudal hierarchy. While literacy rates are relatively high compared to medieval societies, access to advanced education remains a privilege of the samurai and merchant classes. The tension between traditional martial education and rising scholarly pursuits mirrors the broader conflict between old ways and innovation.

## Literacy Rates by Class

### Overall Statistics
- **Total Population Literacy**: Approximately 35-40%
- **Urban Areas**: 50-60% literate
- **Rural Areas**: 20-30% literate
- **Gender Gap**: Male literacy 45%, Female literacy 25%

### By Social Class

**Nobility and Samurai**
- Literacy Rate: 95-100%
- Both men and women educated
- Expected to master:
  - Classical texts
  - Poetry composition
  - Calligraphy
  - Military strategy treatises
  - Historical chronicles

**Priests and Monks**
- Literacy Rate: 100%
- Often the most educated class
- Masters of:
  - Sacred texts
  - Philosophical treatises
  - Astronomical/calendar calculations
  - Medical knowledge
  - Ritual procedures

**Merchants and Artisans**
- Literacy Rate: 60-80%
- Functional literacy focused on:
  - Accounting and record-keeping
  - Contracts and agreements
  - Trade correspondence
  - Technical manuals
  - Price lists and inventories

**Peasants and Laborers**
- Literacy Rate: 15-30%
- Basic literacy when present:
  - Sign names
  - Read simple notices
  - Basic numeracy
  - Village records

## The Writing System

### Script Types

**Kaisho (楷書) - "Standard Script"**
- Formal, block-style characters
- Used for:
  - Official documents
  - Legal contracts
  - Imperial edicts
  - Shogunate decrees
  - Temple inscriptions
- Taught to all samurai children
- Takes years to master

**Gyosho (行書) - "Running Script"**
- Semi-cursive, faster writing
- Used for:
  - Personal correspondence  
  - Poetry
  - Quick notes
  - Merchant records
  - Daily documents
- Standard for educated adults
- Balance of speed and elegance

**Sosho (草書) - "Grass Script"**
- Highly cursive, artistic
- Used for:
  - Calligraphy art
  - Poetic works
  - Personal journals
  - Secret messages (hard to read if unfamiliar)
- Mastery marks high education
- Each calligrapher develops unique style

**Kana (Syllabic Script)**
- Simplified phonetic characters
- Easier to learn than full Kaisho
- Used for:
  - Common communication
  - Women's writing (though educated women also learn Kaisho)
  - Children's early learning
  - Phonetic guides in texts
- Combination of Kaisho and Kana standard in most writing

### Specialized Scripts

**Cipher Scripts**
- Used by ninja clans and Shogunate spies
- Based on Kaisho but with substitutions
- Each organization has unique cipher
- Decryption requires key knowledge

**Runic Scripts**
- Ancient characters used in magical/spiritual contexts
- Onmyoji and priests learn these
- Used for:
  - Spell scrolls
  - Protective talismans
  - Shrine inscriptions
  - Sealing rituals

**Technical Notation**
- Specialized symbols for:
  - Metallurgy (temperature, alloy ratios)
  - Medicine (dosages, symptoms)
  - Architecture (measurements, materials)
  - Military (troop positions, signals)

## Educational Institutions

### Samurai Education

**Domain Academies (Han-ko 藩校)**
- Established by each major daimyo
- Located in castle towns
- Attendance mandatory for samurai youth (ages 7-16)

**Curriculum Includes:**
- Morning: Martial Training (3-4 hours)
  - Swordsmanship
  - Archery
  - Horsemanship
  - Spear work
  - Military strategy
  
- Midday: Scholarly Studies (3-4 hours)
  - Reading classical texts
  - Writing and calligraphy
  - Mathematics and accounting
  - History and ethics
  - Poetry composition
  
- Evening: Specialized Training (1-2 hours)
  - Tea ceremony
  - Music (flute, shamisen)
  - Go and shogi (strategy games)
  - Etiquette and protocol

**Notable Academies:**

*Hikosan Imperial Academy*
- Most prestigious in Shuigari
- Trains sons of high-ranking daimyo
- Direct connection to Shogunate service
- 200 students at any time
- Faculty includes retired generals and scholars
- Graduation rate: 85%

*Takashima Mountain Academy*
- Emphasis on metallurgy and engineering
- Combines martial and technical education
- Students learn smithing alongside strategy
- Famous for producing military engineers
- 150 students
- Graduation rate: 90%

*Harado Mercantile Academy*
- Unusual: Accepts merchant class students alongside samurai
- Focus on administration, logistics, diplomacy
- Controversial but effective
- 180 students
- Graduation rate: 75%

### Temple Schools (Terakoya 寺子屋)

**Structure:**
- Run by Buddhist temples and Shinto shrines
- Open to commoners (small fees)
- Ages 7-14
- Classes held in temple buildings
- 1 teacher per 20-40 students

**Curriculum:**
- Reading (basic Kaisho and Kana)
- Writing (practice with cheap paper, sand trays)
- Arithmetic (abacus, basic math)
- Moral education (religious texts)
- Practical skills (measuring, record-keeping)

**Distribution:**
- Nearly every village has access to terakoya
- Urban areas may have multiple schools
- Quality varies greatly by teacher
- Approximately 40,000 terakoya across Shuigari

**Costs:**
- Tuition: 100-500 mon per year
- Writing materials: 200-400 mon per year
- Additional for calligraphy lessons: 100 mon/month
- Poor families may barter goods/services instead

### Artisan Apprenticeships

**The Apprentice System**
Not formal schools but structured education:

**Age 10-14: Apprentice (Deshi 弟子)**
- Lives with master's family
- Learns basics through observation and menial tasks
- Minimal instruction, mostly imitation
- No pay, receives food/shelter/clothing
- Expected to master foundational skills

**Age 14-20: Journeyman (Shokunin 職人)**
- Practices craft under supervision
- Begins receiving small stipend (500-2,000 mon/month)
- May work for other masters to learn techniques
- Travels to study with specialists
- Produces work that can be sold

**Age 20+: Master (Takumi 匠)**
- Achieves mastery through examination or masterwork creation
- Can establish own workshop
- Takes own apprentices
- Joins guild with full voting rights

**Specialized Schools:**

*Takashima Smithing Guilds*
- Most rigorous weapons training in Shuigari
- 10-year apprenticeship minimum
- Only 1 in 5 apprentices achieve master rank
- Secret techniques passed only to guild members
- Written technical manuals (closely guarded)

*Hikosan Calligraphy Academies*
- Accept both samurai and talented commoners
- 5-8 year training program
- Master calligraphers can earn 20-50 ryo annually
- Prestigious career path for second/third sons

### Ninja Clan Training

**Structure:**
- Closed schools within hidden villages
- Training begins at age 5-6
- Total immersion, no outside contact
- Combines physical, mental, magical training

**Curriculum:**
- Stealth and infiltration
- Disguise and acting
- Poison-craft and medicine
- Cipher and code-breaking
- Athletics and climbing
- Weapons (concealed, improvised)
- Espionage techniques
- Regional dialects and customs

**Literacy Requirements:**
- Must achieve high literacy (multiple scripts)
- Learn specialized cipher systems
- Study psychology and persuasion
- Train in forgery and document creation

**Duration:**
- 10-15 years before first mission
- Lifelong learning and specialization
- Dropout rate: 40% (reassigned to support roles)

### Onmyoji and Magical Training

**Imperial Onmyodo Bureau (Onmyoryo 陰陽寮)**
- Official Shogunate magical institution
- Located in Hikosan
- Highly selective (accepts ~20 students every 3 years)
- Entrance exam tests aptitude for spiritual sensitivity

**Curriculum (8-12 years):**
- Classical cosmology and astrology
- Divination techniques
- Ritual procedures and protocols
- Spirit negotiation and binding
- Protective magic and exorcism
- Calendar creation and astronomy
- Geomancy and feng shui
- Herbalism and alchemy
- Advanced literacy (including runic scripts)

**Graduation:**
- Final exam: Complex divination for Shogunate official
- Success rate: 60%
- Graduates receive rank of Junior Onmyoji
- Assigned to daimyo courts or Shogunate service

**Shrine Priesthood Training**
- Separate from Onmyodo Bureau
- Each major shrine trains own priests/priestesses
- Focus on specific kami traditions
- 5-10 year training period
- Emphasis on ritual purity and procedure
- High literacy in sacred texts required

### Women's Education

**Samurai Daughters**
- Educated at home by tutors
- Focus on:
  - Reading and writing (high level)
  - Calligraphy and poetry
  - Tea ceremony
  - Flower arrangement
  - Music (koto, shamisen)
  - Household management
  - Some martial arts (naginata common)
  - Etiquette and protocol

**Elite Women's Academies**
- Several private institutions in major cities
- Cater to daimyo daughters
- Tuition: 50-200 ryo annually
- Produce highly educated women
- Graduates often become influential through marriage

**Merchant/Artisan Daughters**
- Practical education
- Learn family business
- Basic literacy and numeracy
- Focused on economic independence

**Peasant Women**
- Minimal formal education
- Some terakoya access
- Literacy rate only 15-20%
- Skills passed down mother to daughter

## Libraries and Archives

### Imperial Archives (Hikosan)
- Largest collection in Shuigari
- 50,000+ volumes
- Includes:
  - Historical chronicles
  - Tax records (500+ years)
  - Legal precedents
  - Imperial genealogies
  - Military treatises
- Access: Shogunate officials and approved scholars only

### Domain Libraries
- Each major daimyo maintains collection
- 5,000-20,000 volumes typical
- Focus on domain-specific records:
  - Local history
  - Tax records
  - Land surveys
  - Samurai genealogies
  - Military records

### Temple Libraries
- Buddhist temples often have significant collections
- Medical texts
- Philosophical works
- Historical chronicles
- Popular fiction
- More accessible to public (for donation/fee)

### Merchant Guild Archives
- Trade records and contracts
- Price histories
- Ship manifests
- Credit/debt ledgers
- More recent (last 200 years typically)
- Closely guarded (proprietary information)

## Book Production and Cost

### Manuscript Production

**Hand-Copied Books**
- Monk scribes primary producers
- 1 book takes 2-6 months to copy
- Cost: 5-20 ryo depending on length/complexity
- Only wealthy can afford private collections

**Woodblock Printing**
- Increasingly common in last 100 years
- Faster production once blocks carved
- Popular works: poetry collections, practical manuals
- Cost: 1-5 ryo per printed book
- Making books more accessible

**Materials:**
- Rice paper (washi): 200-500 mon per ream
- Ink: 100-300 mon per stick
- Brushes: 50-500 mon depending on quality
- Binding: 100-200 mon per book

### Popular Literature

**Available to Literate Commoners:**
- Popular tales and folklore: 500-2,000 mon
- Poetry collections: 1-3 shu
- Practical manuals (farming, crafts): 1-2 shu
- Religious texts: Donated by temples (free to copy)

**Samurai Literature:**
- Military treatises: 3-10 bu
- Classical poetry: 2-5 bu
- Historical chronicles: 5-15 bu
- Philosophical works: 3-8 bu

**Rare and Valuable:**
- Ancient manuscripts: 50-500 ryo
- Imperial edicts (originals): Priceless
- Magical grimoires: 100-1,000 ryo
- Master calligraphy: 20-200 ryo

## Specialized Knowledge

### Secret Techniques

**Smithing:**
- Guild secrets passed orally
- Written manuals exist but closely guarded
- Require guild membership to access
- Some techniques known only to 1-2 living masters

**Martial Arts:**
- Sword schools (ryu) maintain scrolls of techniques
- Makimono (scroll) passed headmaster to successor
- Public demonstrations but secrets remain hidden
- Some schools have "hidden techniques" unknown to outsiders

**Onmyoji Magic:**
- Most powerful rituals not written down
- Master-to-student oral transmission
- Written texts often incomplete (intentionally)
- True knowledge requires initiation

## Literacy and Social Mobility

### Education as Advancement

**For Commoners:**
- Literacy opens merchant careers
- Temple school graduates may become:
  - Scribes (500-2,000 mon/month)
  - Teachers (1-3 shu/month)
  - Low-level bureaucrats (2-5 shu/month)
  - Accountants (3-8 shu/month)

**For Lower Samurai:**
- Advanced education necessary for advancement
- Scholarship to prestigious academy changes family fortune
- Literary talent can lead to adviser positions
- Some become professional scholars (5-20 ryo annually)

### The Examination System

**Shogunate Civil Service Exam (Recent Innovation)**
- Introduced 50 years ago
- Controversial (threatens hereditary privilege)
- Open to samurai class only
- Held every 3 years in Hikosan
- Tests:
  - Classical literature
  - History
  - Ethics and philosophy
  - Poetry composition
  - Administrative law
  - Mathematics

**Success Rates:**
- Only 10-15% pass
- Successful candidates guaranteed Shogunate position
- Positions: 5-20 koku stipend
- Path to higher office
- Some daimyo conduct own exams

## Current Educational Trends

### Tensions

**Traditionalist View:**
- Too much literacy threatens social order
- Commoners should focus on their station
- Classical education sufficient for samurai
- New subjects (foreign knowledge) corrupting

**Reformist View:**
- Shuigari falling behind
- Need technical education (engineering, medicine)
- Merit should matter more than birth
- Expand examination system

### Emerging Changes

**Western Learning (Rangaku 蘭学)**
- Small but growing interest in foreign knowledge
- Mathematics and science from across Sea of Mirrors
- Controversial (foreign = impure to many)
- Secret study circles in port cities
- Some progressive daimyo sponsor research

**Expanded Women's Education**
- More academies opening
- Debate: Does female education destabilize families?
- Some women becoming published poets/authors
- Still far from equality but changing

**Merchant Academies**
- Teaching administration, accounting, foreign languages
- Filling gap in Shogunate education
- Wealthy merchants funding these
- Graduates often more practical than samurai academy students

- Magical grimoire stolen from temple library

### PC Backgrounds
- Former samurai academy student (expelled for?)
- Temple school teacher turned adventurer

- Merchant guild scholar with forbidden knowledge
- Ninja dropout with incomplete training

### Knowledge Skills
Use education background to determine what PCs know:
- Samurai academy: History, military strategy, etiquette
- Temple school: Basic literacy, local lore, religion
- Artisan apprentice: Trade techniques, guild politics
- Ninja training: Espionage, poison-craft, disguise
- Onmyoji training: Spirits, rituals, astrology

- Rare books and scrolls
- Secret technique manual
- Forged documents
- Library access
- Tuition for follower/hireling
