# Furenhoku

## Kiyama

[h2]Kiyama: The Mountain’s Forge[/h2]<br />Kiyama is a remote mining town situated deep in the Frozen Peaks of Furenhoku. It is known as the “Mountain’s Forge” due to its harsh environment and the wealth of rare minerals extracted from its icy depths. The town is a stark contrast to the coastal settlements of the island, with its jagged cliffs, snow-covered paths, and constant chill in the air. Despite the grueling conditions, Kiyama is a vital part of Furenhoku’s economy, providing the iron, silver, and the highly sought-after Frostiron used in the forges of Hokushu Fort and beyond.<br /><br />Perched on the side of a steep mountain pass, Kiyama is built more like a fortress than a traditional town. Its wooden and stone structures cling to the rocky terrain, connected by narrow bridges and stairways carved into the rock. The surrounding cliffs are dotted with torch-lit watchtowers and signal fires that guide miners and traders through the treacherous landscape. Kiyama is a town of grim determination, where only the toughest survive.<br /><br />[h2]Strategic Importance[/h2]<br />Kiyama’s mines produce some of the finest metals in the entire Shuigari Archipelago, including the rare Frostiron, a metal infused with the essence of ice. Weapons and armor forged from Frostiron are said to retain a chilling aura that can freeze opponents with a single touch, making them highly prized by warriors and smiths alike. The wealth generated by these resources not only funds Furenhoku’s defenses but also attracts ambitious prospectors and traders from across the archipelago.<br /><br />In addition to its economic role, Kiyama serves as a strategic outpost for Hokushu Fort. Its location high in the Frozen Peaks makes it a natural stronghold against any northern invaders. The town’s Mountain Guard patrol the high passes, ensuring that the mines are protected and that no enemy can use the rugged terrain to bypass the fort’s defenses.<br /><br />[h2]Governance and Defense[/h2]<br />Kiyama is ruled by Master Ishida Noboru, a stern and pragmatic overseer appointed by Lord Ieyasu Tsukino. He is responsible for maintaining order in the mines and ensuring the steady flow of resources to Hokushu Fort. Though he is not a warrior, Ishida’s keen mind and unflinching resolve have made him a respected leader among the miners and soldiers stationed here.<br /><br />The town’s defenses are formidable, despite its small size:<br /><br />	•	The Iron Gate: A massive iron-reinforced gate at the entrance of the main mining complex. It can be sealed shut during an attack, making the town almost impregnable from the outside.<br />	•	Mountain Guard Patrols: The Mountain Guard, a specialized force of 200 soldiers, patrols the high passes and guards the key mining sites. Their knowledge of the terrain and skill in mountain combat make them a formidable force against intruders.<br />	•	Frostwatch Tower: The highest watchtower in the town, equipped with a massive signal fire that can be seen for miles. It serves as both a lookout and a rallying point for the defenders of Kiyama.<br /><br />[h2]Town Layout and Districts[/h2]<br /><br />Kiyama is divided into three main districts, each adapted to the unique challenges of life in the Frozen Peaks:<br /><br />**1. The Mining District:**<br />The heart of Kiyama’s economy, the Mining District is a sprawling network of tunnels, shafts, and smelting forges that descend deep into the mountain. The largest mine, Ishikawa’s Vein, is said to contain the purest deposits of Frostiron in all of Furenhoku. The district is constantly alive with the sounds of pickaxes, the roar of the forges, and the creaking of mine carts as workers labor to extract the precious ore.<br /><br />**2. The Worker’s Quarter:**<br />A collection of rugged stone and timber houses, the Worker’s Quarter is where the miners and their families live. Life here is harsh, with long work hours and little comfort, but the sense of community is strong. The central square, known as Ironheart Plaza, is the only open space in the town, where the locals gather for festivals and to share stories around the communal fire.<br /><br />**3. The Overseer’s Hall:**<br />Perched on a high ledge overlooking the rest of the town, the Overseer’s Hall is both the residence of Master Ishida and the administrative center of Kiyama. It is here that the ore shipments are tallied, disputes are settled, and plans for the town’s expansion are made. The Hall also houses the offices of the Miner’s Guild, which represents the interests of the workers and ensures that safety standards are maintained.<br /><br />[h2]Economy and Resources[/h2]<br />Kiyama’s wealth comes primarily from its rich deposits of minerals, especially Frostiron. The ore is smelted in the town’s forges and then transported to Hokushu Fort, where it is used to craft weapons, armor, and other goods that are then shipped across the archipelago. In addition to Frostiron, Kiyama also produces:<br /><br />	•	Silver and Iron: Essential for crafting and trade, these metals are exported to support the war effort and to maintain the fortifications of the Shogunate.<br />	•	Rare Crystals: Occasionally found deep within the mines, these crystals are valued by enchanters and alchemists for their unique properties.<br /><br />[h2]Current Political Climate[/h2]<br />Despite its wealth, Kiyama is a town under constant pressure. Recent cave-ins and increased attacks from hostile yokai have disrupted mining operations, raising tensions between the workers and the overseers. There are also rumors of a growing discontent among the miners, who feel that their sacrifices are not adequately compensated. If unrest were to break out, it could threaten not only the town’s stability but also the supply lines vital to Furenhoku’s defenses.<br /><br />Lord Ieyasu is aware of these tensions and has begun sending additional troops to Kiyama, ostensibly to defend against external threats. However, some believe that the real reason is to keep a closer watch on Master Ishida and ensure that the town remains under the Shogunate’s control.

## Umiwari

[h2]Umiwari: The Sea Guardian of Furenhoku[/h2]<br />Umiwari is a fortified coastal town located on the rugged western shores of Furenhoku. Often called the “Guardian of the Frozen Waves”, Umiwari serves as the island’s main naval outpost and its first line of defense against the harsh northern seas. It is a place where icy winds howl through narrow streets, and the constant roar of the ocean reminds its people of the perilous waters they call home. Known for its vigilant watchtowers, bustling fish markets, and skilled mariners, Umiwari is a town hardened by the relentless tides and the ever-present threat of raiders and sea yokai.<br /><br />The town’s skyline is dominated by the towering Sea Watch Citadel, a massive stone lighthouse and fortress combined, which stands as both a beacon for sailors and a symbol of Umiwari’s unyielding resolve. From here, Lord Daigo Arashi, the Commander of the Northern Fleet, oversees the defense of the coastal regions, coordinating patrols and maintaining a tight grip on all maritime activity around Furenhoku.<br /><br />[h2]Strategic Importance[/h2]<br />Umiwari’s position on Furenhoku’s western coast is critical. It sits at the mouth of the Ishikawa Strait, one of the few navigable routes through the treacherous waters of the northern archipelago. Controlling this passage allows Umiwari to regulate all sea traffic, making it a vital checkpoint for both trade and military operations. The town’s Harbor Guard patrols these waters, scanning the horizon for signs of pirates, smugglers, or worse—raiding fleets from hostile northern clans.<br /><br />With its fleet of fishing boats, merchant vessels, and warships, Umiwari is also the logistical backbone of Furenhoku’s coastal settlements. In times of war, its ships transport troops and supplies to reinforce Hokushu Fort and other key locations. During peace, Umiwari thrives as a center of maritime commerce, its markets filled with the bounty of the sea.<br /><br />[h2]Governance and Defense[/h2]<br />Umiwari is governed by Lord Daigo Arashi, a seasoned naval commander known for his sharp tactical mind and unflinching dedication to the town’s defense. His residence, the Storm’s Roost, is a fortified manor overlooking the harbor, from where he issues orders and receives emissaries from other parts of Furenhoku. While technically a vassal of Lord Ieyasu Tsukino of Hokushu Fort, Lord Daigo’s authority over Umiwari and its fleet gives him significant autonomy, making him one of the most powerful figures in the region.<br /><br />The town’s defenses are robust, designed to withstand both seaborne assaults and land invasions:<br /><br />	•	Sea Watch Citadel: The central fortress and lighthouse, equipped with ballistae, signal fires, and enchanted lanterns that can pierce the thickest fog. The citadel’s watchtower offers a panoramic view of the entire coastline, allowing the Harbor Guard to spot enemy ships long before they reach the shore.<br />	•	Harbor Wall: A thick stone wall encircling Umiwari’s harbor, lined with cannons and watchtowers. The gates can be sealed off in times of attack, turning the harbor into a fortified bay.<br />	•	The Chain of Kurogane: A massive iron chain stretched across the harbor entrance, which can be raised to block the passage of enemy ships. The chain is enchanted to resist corrosion and break under immense stress, making it a nearly impregnable barrier.<br /><br />[h2]Town Layout and Districts[/h2]<br /><br />Umiwari is divided into several districts, each serving a specific purpose that supports the town’s economy and defense:<br /><br />**1. The Harbor District:**<br />The heart of Umiwari’s economy, the Harbor District is a bustling maze of piers, warehouses, and fish markets. The docks are filled with ships of all sizes, from humble fishing boats to war galleys bristling with cannons. The Fishmonger’s Market is the busiest part of the district, where the catch of the day—crabs, seaweed, and the prized “Frost Koi”—is sold and traded. The Harbor Guard maintains a strong presence here, ensuring that all maritime activity is carefully monitored.<br /><br />**2. The Citadel Ward:**<br />Centered around the imposing Sea Watch Citadel, this district is the administrative and military heart of Umiwari. The Commander’s Hall, barracks, and armories are all located here, as well as the training grounds for Umiwari’s elite Stormblade Marines. Access is heavily restricted, and only those with official business or military rank are permitted inside the Citadel Ward.<br /><br />**3. The Fisher’s Quarter:**<br />A dense network of narrow streets and low wooden buildings, the Fisher’s Quarter is where most of Umiwari’s common folk live and work. Homes are built close together, their rooftops lined with drying racks for fish and seaweed. The Shrine of the Ocean’s Grace is the spiritual center of this district, dedicated to the sea kami that protect the town’s fishermen. Rituals and offerings are made daily to ensure safe voyages and bountiful catches.<br /><br />**4. The Merchant’s Row:**<br />Located just behind the Harbor District, the Merchant’s Row is where traders from across the archipelago come to barter and sell their wares. From exotic spices to enchanted talismans, the goods found here are diverse and often smuggled in through less-than-legal channels. The Trader’s Guild exerts considerable influence over the district, and rumors say that some of its members are involved in smuggling and black market deals.<br /><br />[h2]Economy and Influence[/h2]<br />Umiwari’s economy is centered around its control over the Ishikawa Strait and its abundant fishing grounds. The town’s merchants have lucrative contracts with inland settlements, providing dried and salted fish, whale oil, and valuable seaweed to the rest of Furenhoku. The town is also known for its shipwrights, who build sturdy warships and swift trading vessels capable of navigating the treacherous northern waters.<br /><br />Umiwari’s influence extends beyond its shores due to its naval power. Lord Daigo often deploys his fleet to support other coastal settlements or to deal with pirate activity, making him a key player in Furenhoku’s security and politics.<br /><br />[h2]Current Political Climate[/h2]<br />Although outwardly loyal to Hokushu Fort, Lord Daigo’s increasing autonomy has begun to raise concerns among Lord Ieyasu’s inner circle. Some whisper that Daigo might be considering consolidating power along the coast, potentially challenging the authority of Hokushu Fort. Meanwhile, the town’s proximity to the hostile northern islands and frequent encounters with raiding ships have made some of Umiwari’s people weary of endless vigilance and hardship. The question remains: how long can Umiwari maintain its unbroken watch before its people seek a different path?

## Hokushu Fort

[h2]Hokushu Fort: The Bastion of the North[/h2]<br />Hokushu Fort is the largest and most fortified stronghold on the island of Furenhoku, standing as a bulwark against the harsh northern elements and foreign threats. Situated atop the Icy Crag, a high vantage point overlooking the main fjords and passes, Hokushu Fort serves as both a military headquarters and the de facto capital of the island. Its imposing black stone walls are reinforced with iron, and its towers are equipped with ballistae to ward off both human and yokai invaders.<br /><br />Unlike the grand cities of Shugane, Hokushu Fort is a testament to function over form, designed to endure the extreme weather and constant skirmishes that define life in the north. It is home to the Warlord of Furenhoku, the regional daimyo who commands the island’s defenses and oversees the scattered settlements across the treacherous terrain. The fort’s reputation as an impenetrable bastion has earned it the title, “The Shield of Shuigari”.<br /><br />[h2]Strategic Importance[/h2]<br />Hokushu Fort’s location is key to maintaining order in Furenhoku. From its high walls, one can see the entire northern coastline and the entrances to the island’s major fjords. It controls the Kenshou Pass, the only accessible route to the interior highlands, making it the last line of defense against any would-be invaders from the northern seas or neighboring territories.<br /><br />The fort also serves as the primary rallying point for the island’s militia, housing barracks, armories, and a large central courtyard used for training. It is said that the cold winds that whip through Hokushu’s walls are as much a part of the fort’s defenses as its soldiers, hardening any who dwell within its walls.<br /><br />[h2]The Seat of Power[/h2]<br />Hokushu Fort is ruled by Lord Ieyasu Tsukino, the Warlord of Furenhoku, a stern and calculating leader known for his unyielding resolve and military acumen. His command center, located in the Wolf’s Keep at the heart of the fort, is heavily guarded and filled with maps, strategic plans, and correspondences from his vassals scattered across the island. Despite his harsh reputation, Lord Ieyasu is respected for his ability to keep the unruly northern tribes in check and maintain a fragile peace in the region.<br /><br />[h2]Fort Layout and Districts[/h2]<br /><br />Hokushu Fort is divided into several key sections, each designed to support the military and administrative needs of Furenhoku:<br /><br />**1. The Wolf’s Keep:**<br />The central keep of Hokushu Fort, where Lord Ieyasu resides and commands. It is a towering structure with reinforced walls, housing both his personal quarters and the main war room. The keep is equipped with reinforced iron gates, making it nearly impregnable in times of siege.<br /><br />**2. The Barracks:**<br />A sprawling complex where the soldiers of Furenhoku live, train, and prepare for battle. The barracks are large enough to accommodate over 1,500 troops, with dedicated training grounds and armories filled with weapons and supplies. The harsh training regimens are legendary, ensuring that only the strongest and most disciplined warriors defend the fort.<br /><br />**3. The Forge District:**<br />This section of the fort houses the forges and workshops where the weapons and armor for Furenhoku’s warriors are crafted. The blacksmiths of Hokushu Fort are known for their skill in working with the rare Frostiron ore mined from the Frozen Peaks, producing weapons that are both durable and capable of withstanding the harsh cold. The “Winter’s Edge”, a famous type of katana forged here, is said to cut through both steel and ice with ease.<br /><br />**4. The Outer Wall and Watchtowers:**<br />The outer defenses of Hokushu Fort are formidable, consisting of thick stone walls reinforced with iron bands. The watchtowers are equipped with ballistae, signal fires, and even enchanted braziers that burn with blue flames, warding off malevolent yokai. The towers provide a clear line of sight over the fjords and valleys, allowing the defenders to spot approaching threats from miles away.<br /><br />**5. The Courtyard:**<br />A large, open area at the center of the fort used for military drills, parades, and ceremonial events. The harsh, freezing winds of Furenhoku often sweep through the courtyard, adding an extra layer of challenge to the training of new recruits. During important gatherings, the courtyard is adorned with banners representing the various clans loyal to Lord Ieyasu.<br /><br />**6. The Shrine of the Wolf’s Spirit:**<br />A small but sacred shrine dedicated to the Wolf Spirit of the North, the kami believed to watch over the fort and its warriors. The shrine is maintained by a small order of monks and priests, who perform rituals to honor the spirit and seek its blessing for strength and protection. During times of war, the Warlord and his generals often pray here before battle.<br /><br />[h2]Economy and Resources[/h2]<br />While primarily a military fort, Hokushu also serves as an administrative center for the surrounding region, overseeing the collection of resources from the rest of Furenhoku. The main sources of wealth include:<br /><br /> • Frostiron Mining: The mines near Kiyama provide Frostiron and other rare minerals, making the forge district vital to the island’s economy.<br />• Timber and Lumber: Logging from the nearby Spirit Woods supplies the fort with materials for construction and fuel.<br />• Fishing and Whaling: Coastal settlements like Umiwari and Kazehama send regular shipments of fish, whale oil, and preserved seafood to the fort, sustaining its soldiers and craftsmen.<br /><br />The fort’s strategic control over these resources allows Lord Ieyasu to maintain a tight grip on the economy and ensure the loyalty of his vassals.<br /><br />[h2]Current Political Climate[/h2]<br />Hokushu Fort is a bastion of order in an otherwise wild and chaotic land. However, tensions have been rising recently. The northern tribes, emboldened by a series of harsh winters and yokai disturbances, have begun to test the fort’s defenses with small raids and skirmishes. There are also whispers of discontent among some of Lord Ieyasu’s vassals, who resent the strict control imposed from the fort and seek greater autonomy.<br /><br />In the midst of this unrest, rumors have spread of a new warlord rising in the distant north—one who claims to be the true heir of Furenhoku’s ancient kings. Should this figure unite the tribes, Hokushu Fort may find itself besieged by an enemy not seen in generations. Lord Ieyasu must tread carefully, balancing between maintaining the fort’s defenses and quelling internal dissent before it’s too late.

## Towns of Furenhoku

The towns of Furenhoku are few and far between, scattered across the island’s frozen peaks and icy fjords. Each town must contend with the harsh environment, constant threats from yokai, and isolation. Despite these challenges, the people of Furenhoku have built resilient communities that thrive on the island’s unique resources. Below is an overview of the notable towns in Furenhoku, highlighting their role, strategic importance, and what they are known for.<br /><br />[h2]1. Kogamatsu (Port Town, Eastern Coast)[/h2]<br />**Population:** 5,000.<br />**Defenses:** Wooden palisades and a naval garrison of 150 soldiers.<br />**Commerce:** Specializes in whaling, seal hunting, and the trade of rare oceanic products.<br />**Organizations:** The Kogamatsu Whalers’ Guild and the Order of the Stormy Seas oversee maritime activities.<br /><br />Kogamatsu is Furenhoku’s largest port and the primary center of trade with the other islands. The town’s whalers and seal hunters are known for braving the dangerous icy waters of the Sea of Mirrors.<br /><br />[h2]2. Yureizato (Mountain Town, Frozen Peaks)[/h2]<br />**Population:** 3,200.<br />**Defenses:** High stone walls and 200 defenders guard this remote mountain town.<br />**Commerce:** Known for mining ghostly crystals and rare enchanted ice.<br />**Organizations:** The Frostbound Miners’ Guild and the Order of the Frozen Veil.<br /><br />Yureizato is nestled high in the Frozen Peaks, shrouded in perpetual snowstorms. The town is famous for its eerie crystals, which are rumored to capture the spirits of the dead.<br /><br />[h2]3. Hamayumi (Fishing Town, Southern Fjords)[/h2]<br />**Population:** 4,600.<br />**Defenses:** Wooden barricades and a small militia of 100 warriors.<br />**Commerce:** Known for smoked fish, shellfish, and whale oil production.<br />**Organizations:** The Hamayumi Fishermen’s Guild and the Tidecaller’s Circle.<br /><br />Hamayumi’s fishermen are renowned for their skill in navigating the treacherous fjords of Furenhoku. The town’s smoked seafood is highly sought after across the archipelago.<br /><br />[h2]4. Kazemura (Fortified Town, Northern Highlands)[/h2]<br />**Population:** 7,200.<br />**Defenses:** Thick stone walls and a garrison of 500 soldiers.<br />**Commerce:** Produces high-quality furs, leather, and cold-weather gear.<br />**Organizations:** The Kazemura Trappers’ Guild and the House of the Winter Wolf.<br /><br />Kazemura is Furenhoku’s primary defensive stronghold in the north. The town’s hunters and trappers are famed for their prowess in capturing the dangerous beasts that roam the highlands.<br /><br />[h2]5. Isejima (Monastery Town, Western Foothills)[/h2]<br />**Population:** 3,400.<br />**Defenses:** Stone walls and a monastery militia of 200 warrior monks.<br />**Commerce:** Known for its herbal medicines and spiritual texts.<br />**Organizations:** The Order of Tranquil Snow and the Monastery of the Silent Peak.<br /><br />Isejima is a tranquil town built around a secluded monastery. The monks here are respected for their wisdom and mastery over elemental ice magic, and the town is a center for spiritual pilgrimage.<br /><br />[h2]6. Yoruhara (Fortified Town, Spirit Woods)[/h2]<br />**Population:** 6,800.<br />**Defenses:** High wooden palisades and 300 archers.<br />**Commerce:** Specializes in logging and herbalism.<br />**Organizations:** The Yoruhara Foresters’ Guild and the Circle of Verdant Spirits.<br /><br />Yoruhara is located on the edge of the haunted Spirit Woods, where yokai and restless spirits are known to wander. The town’s herbalists harvest rare plants that are believed to grow only with the blessing of the forest kami.<br /><br />[h2]7. Kamikawa (Mining Town, Central Glaciers)[/h2]<br />**Population:** 5,300.<br />**Defenses:** Reinforced stone walls and 150 guards.<br />**Commerce:** Known for silver and iron mining, as well as the production of enchanted weapons.<br />**Organizations:** The Kamikawa Mining Consortium and the Silver Forge Brotherhood.<br /><br />Kamikawa is a mining town built into the icy caverns beneath Furenhoku’s central glaciers. The town’s blacksmiths are famous for crafting enchanted weapons that harness the elemental power of the ice.<br /><br />[h2]8. Shiratori (Border Town, Northwestern Coast)[/h2]<br />**Population:** 4,900.<br />**Defenses:** Strong wooden walls and a garrison of 200 soldiers.<br />**Commerce:** Primarily a trade hub and defensive outpost.<br />**Organizations:** The Shiratori Guard and the Northern Trade Alliance.<br /><br />Shiratori is a vital border town and trading post along the Sea of Mirrors. The town’s walls have withstood many raids, making it an essential defensive point on Furenhoku’s northwestern coastline.
