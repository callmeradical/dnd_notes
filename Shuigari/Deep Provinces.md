## Okugōri (Deep Provinces)

In the rugged wilderness of [[Takashima Prefecture]], shrouded in mist and mystery, lie the **Okugōri (Deep Provinces)**—hidden enclaves of ancient martial and spiritual traditions. These sanctuaries were established by rogue clans, wandering mystics, and outcast samurai during the chaotic Age of Warring Daimyos. Over centuries, they have evolved into secretive hubs of power, guided by a delicate balance between the mortal and the spiritual realms.

The Okugōri are fiercely protective of their independence, hiding from the prying eyes of the Shogunate and feudal lords. Each province is unique, shaped by the natural and spiritual forces of its environment, and their shinobi (known collectively as **Gōrijin, "People of the Deep Provinces"**) are renowned for their unparalleled skills in war, espionage, and mysticism.

## The Three Great Ranks of the Gōrijin

The Okugōri measure progression through ranks that symbolize spiritual growth, martial refinement, and an increasingly intimate connection to the kami. These ranks are recognized across all provinces, though the specific trials and ceremonies to ascend vary by region.

### Tsukiwaka (月若, "Moonling")

- **Rank:** Apprentice
- **Role:** Novices who have begun their journey into the mystical and martial arts of the Okugōri. They are compared to the crescent moon—fragile, untested, yet full of potential to grow and shine.
- **Duties:**
  - Basic espionage, courier work, and assisting higher-ranked Gōrijin.
  - Learning the fundamental arts of the province, such as hand-to-hand combat, spiritual rituals, and elemental techniques.
  - Completing physical and spiritual endurance tests to build resilience.
- **Symbolism:** The crescent moon represents both new beginnings and the unseen—traits critical to a shinobi's survival.
- **Typical Gear:** Simple robes, kunai, and training tools. No province crest is worn until promotion.

### Taiyōshi (太陽子, "Child of the Sun")

- **Rank:** Journeyman
- **Role:** Fully trained shinobi who serve as the backbone of the province. Taiyōshi are warriors and operatives skilled in combat, infiltration, and diplomacy. They are considered like the rising sun—bright, capable, and able to stand on their own.
- **Duties:**
  - Leading small teams on missions, including sabotage, reconnaissance, and protection of the Okugōri's secrets.
  - Mastering at least one advanced discipline (such as stealth, elemental manipulation, or ritual exorcism).
  - Mentoring Tsukiwaka to guide their early growth.
- **Symbolism:** The sun signifies power, clarity, and maturity—traits expected of a Taiyōshi.
- **Typical Gear:** Custom armor and weapons crafted to reflect their chosen discipline. They proudly wear their province's crest on their forehead protector, armor, or robes.

### Rairyū (雷龍, "Thunder Dragon")

- **Rank:** Master
- **Role:** The elite among the Gōrijin, Rairyū are living legends within their provinces, their strength and wisdom akin to dragons commanding storms. They act as generals, spiritual leaders, and enforcers of the Okugōri's will. Few ever reach this rank, and those who do are revered as avatars of their province's ideals.
- **Duties:**
  - Leading large-scale operations or defending the province during times of crisis.
  - Teaching advanced techniques to Taiyōshi and developing new ones that blend their personal style with their province's unique traditions.
  - Serving as emissaries or negotiators when the Okugōri must interact with the outside world.
- **Symbolism:** The thunder dragon represents overwhelming power, wisdom, and the divine—embodying the apex of what it means to be Gōrijin.
- **Typical Gear:** Ornate armor and enchanted weapons, often forged from rare materials or imbued with the spiritual essence of their province's kami. Their gear is said to be as fearsome as their reputation.

## Optional Specialized Rank: Yamikami (闇神, "Shadow Kami")

- **Rank:** Secret Operative
- **Role:** Yamikami are the shadowy elite—assassins, spies, and saboteurs who operate entirely in secrecy, even within their own province. They are seen as divine enforcers of balance, working unseen to eliminate threats and uphold their province's security. Some say Yamikami do not truly exist, but whispers persist that they are chosen directly by the kami.
- **Symbolism:** The Shadow Kami's existence reflects the duality of creation and destruction, order and chaos. They are the sword and the shield in perfect harmony.

## Rituals of Advancement

Advancing through the ranks is not simply a matter of skill; it requires trials that test not only the physical and mental fortitude of the shinobi but also their spiritual connection to their province's kami.

1. **Tsukiwaka to Taiyōshi – "The Rite of the Rising Sun":** This trial often involves a week-long solo mission in hostile territory, a test of survival and resourcefulness. Upon success, the initiate is presented with the crest of their Okugōri and sworn in as a Taiyōshi.
2. **Taiyōshi to Rairyū – "The Dragon's Ascent":** This trial varies by province but usually involves facing a dangerous foe or performing a heroic deed that brings honor to the province. Many Rairyū must also demonstrate mastery over their kami's favored element.
3. **Becoming Yamikami – "The Shadow's Whisper":** There is no formal trial; the Yamikami are chosen in secret by the Kage or the province's kami themselves. Those chosen disappear for months or years, returning as silent, ghost-like figures of unimaginable skill.
