# The Worship of Tsukuyomi

The worship of **Tsukuyomi** in Shuigari is nuanced, reflecting both reverence and fear, much like the dynamic between mortals and **Hades** in Greek mythology. Here's an exploration of Tsukuyomi's place in the spiritual and cultural fabric of Shuigari:

## Tsukuyomi: The Ambiguous Moon Kami

### Dual Perception

- **Reverence:** Tsukuyomi is recognized as the **Hidden Moon Kami**, embodying secrets, transitions, and balance between light and darkness. His domain over the moon ties him to cycles of change, which are essential for life, such as tides, growth, and decay. His festival, **Tsukuyomi's Night**, celebrates these cycles and their mysterious beauty.
- **Fear:** Conversely, Tsukuyomi's lore paints him as a *dangerous arbiter of cosmic justice*. His actions are often interpreted as cold, calculated, and without regard for mortal life. Legends describe him as exacting vengeance on mortals and kami alike, should they upset the natural order.

This duality makes Tsukuyomi both venerated and feared—a kami whose favor is sought cautiously and whose wrath is meticulously avoided.

## Why Mortals Are Wary of Tsukuyomi

1. **The Banished Moon:**  
   Tsukuyomi is said to have severed ties with the other celestial kami after committing an act of divine violence. One version of the myth tells of him slaying a harvest kami, Uke Mochi, for presenting an offering he deemed disrespectful. This act shattered his relationship with the sun kami, Amateru no Kaze, and cast him as a loner, distant and cold.

2. **His Role as a Cosmic Judge:**  
   Tsukuyomi is often seen as a kami of karmic balance, punishing mortals and spirits alike for transgressions. His judgment is absolute, and his methods—often subtle and inscrutable—leave lasting fear in their wake. Stories tell of entire villages cursed with barren fields or unnatural silence after offending him.

3. **Secrets and Shadows:**  
   As the Hidden Moon Kami, Tsukuyomi is tied to the unknown. He is invoked in times of uncertainty or when dealing with hidden truths, but his involvement is feared for the consequences it might bring. Those who pray to Tsukuyomi for knowledge risk losing more than they gain.

## Tsukuyomi's Worship: Public vs. Private

### Public Worship (The Festival of Tsukuyomi's Night)

- During **Tsukuyomi's Night**, villagers and monks gather to honor the moon's mysterious power. This festival centers on balance and reflection:
  - **Moon Viewing:** Silent moon-viewing ceremonies encourage participants to meditate on hidden truths.
  - **Offerings of White Rice and Sake:** Pure offerings are left at shrines to appease Tsukuyomi and ward off his wrath.
  - **Lantern Ceremonies:** Spirit lanterns are floated on rivers to represent the boundary between light and darkness, mortal and divine.
- The festival is celebratory yet subdued, marked by an undercurrent of caution. Few invoke Tsukuyomi's name lightly, and the festivities often end with prayers asking him to "remain in balance."

### Private Worship (Fearful Devotion)

- Individual worship of Tsukuyomi is rare and often done out of necessity rather than love. Those who seek his favor are typically desperate:
  - **Travelers and Thieves:** Pray for protection under the cover of night.
  - **Scholars and Mystics:** Seek guidance in uncovering hidden knowledge or solving riddles.
  - **Oathbound Seekers:** Offer prayers before undertaking tasks of karmic retribution, aligning themselves with Tsukuyomi's sense of justice.
- These prayers are given in private, often accompanied by rituals to cleanse the soul, for fear of drawing too much of Tsukuyomi's attention.

## Comparison to Hades

- Like Hades, Tsukuyomi isn't inherently evil but is misunderstood due to his association with dark or fearsome elements.
- Mortals fear him because of his unyielding, impartial nature. He isn't swayed by mortal emotions, and his actions often seem harsh or unforgiving.
- Unlike Hades, who oversees the underworld, Tsukuyomi governs the unseen aspects of the cosmos—secrets, shadows, and the balance between light and dark. This ambiguity amplifies the mystique and fear surrounding him.

## Mythological and Cultural Notes

1. **Ambiguous Blessings:**  
   Tsukuyomi's blessings are as feared as his curses. Farmers might pray for a bountiful harvest under the moonlight but wonder if they'll be cursed for an imperfect offering.

2. **Tsukuyomi's Influence on Yokai:**  
   Tsukuyomi is said to have a tenuous connection with certain yokai, especially those tied to the night and shadows. Villagers speak of yokai who serve as his messengers, delivering cryptic warnings or punishments.

3. **Tsukuyomi and the Silver Road:**  
   The **Silver Road** is often associated with the moon due to its pale, reflective surface under moonlight. Some travelers believe Tsukuyomi protects those who walk the road in peace but curses those who tread it with ill intent.

## How This Impacts the World and Campaign

- **Ambiguity in Worship:** The party may encounter NPCs who view Tsukuyomi differently. Some might speak of him as a protector, while others whisper about his wrath.
- **Lore in Action:** The Festival of Tsukuyomi's Night could set the stage for hidden truths to be revealed, secrets to be unearthed, or yokai tied to Tsukuyomi to test the party.
- **Player Dilemma:** If the party must seek Tsukuyomi's favor, they will need to balance reverence with fear, knowing that his gifts come with a price.
