## Villages of Furenhoku

1. Kazehama<br /><br />[ul]<br />[li]**Fishing village, northeastern coast.**[/li]<br />[li]**Population:** 350.[/li]<br />[li]**Known for:** Kazehama is renowned for its whaling traditions. The village’s whale oil and blubber are key resources for the local economy, and the villagers believe that their whaling ships are protected by the Sea Spirit, Umikami. The annual *“Feast of the Great Whale”* is a time of celebration, where the villagers honor the spirits of the sea for providing their bounty.[/li]<br />[/ul]<br /><br />2. Mizuta<br /><br />[ul]<br />[li]**River village, central Furenhoku.**[/li]<br />[li]**Population:** 400.[/li]<br />[li]**Known for:** Situated along the Ibaraki River, Mizuta is a small logging and fishing village. The proximity to the Spirit Woods means that the villagers are deeply superstitious, performing daily rituals to appease the forest spirits. They are also known for their unique wooden carvings, believed to contain protective charms against malevolent yokai.[/li]<br />[/ul]<br /><br />3. Rokkaku<br /><br />[ul]<br />[li]**Mountain village, southern Frozen Peaks.**[/li]<br />[li]**Population:** 250.[/li]<br />[li]**Known for:** High in the Frozen Peaks, Rokkaku is a place of isolation and resilience. The villagers are expert mountaineers and often guide travelers seeking to cross the dangerous mountain passes. The village is also known for its small shrine to the Mountain Kami, where offerings are made to ensure safe passage and protection from avalanches.[/li]<br />[/ul]<br /><br />4. Shirakawa<br /><br />[ul]<br />[li]**Forest village, deep within the Spirit Woods.**[/li]<br />[li]**Population:** 150.[/li]<br />[li]**Known for:** A mysterious village shrouded in mist and legend, Shirakawa is said to exist on the border between the mortal realm and the spirit world. The villagers are reputed to be mediums and exorcists, communing with the restless spirits that haunt the woods. The village’s annual *“Night of Veils”* is when the boundaries between worlds are thinnest, and the people offer lanterns to guide lost souls back to their resting places.[/li]<br />[/ul]<br /><br />5. Toriyama<br /><br />[ul]<br />[li]**Fortified village, southern foothills.**[/li]<br />[li]**Population:** 500.[/li]<br />[li]**Known for:** Toriyama is a fortified outpost built to defend against potential incursions from the mainland. The village is surrounded by stone walls, and its people are primarily soldiers and their families. Known as the “Iron Gate of Furenhoku,” Toriyama’s martial prowess is respected across the island. The *“Trial of Blades”*, a local martial arts tournament, draws fighters from across Furenhoku.[/li]<br />[/ul]<br /><br />6. Yamagata<br /><br />[ul]<br />[li]**Mountain village, central highlands.**[/li]<br />[li]**Population:** 300.[/li]<br />[li]**Known for:** Yamagata is a quiet, secluded village famous for its hot springs, which are believed to have healing properties. The villagers are deeply spiritual, and the springs are considered a sacred site dedicated to the Dragon Kami, who is said to bless the waters. Many come to Yamagata seeking both physical and spiritual healing.[/li]<br />[/ul]<br /><br />7. Okuma<br /><br />[ul]<br />[li]**Mining village, northern slopes of the Frozen Peaks.**[/li]<br />[li]**Population:** 450.[/li]<br />[li]**Known for:** Okuma is a mining village known for its deposits of Frostiron and other rare minerals. Life here is harsh and dangerous, with miners constantly facing the risk of cave-ins and yokai attacks. The village is fortified, and its inhabitants are a hardy people, accustomed to enduring the bitter cold and the ever-present threat of the mountain spirits’ wrath.[/li]<br />[/ul]<br /><br />8. Shimozawa<br /><br />[ul]<br />[li]**Fishing village, western fjords.**[/li]<br />[li]**Population:** 300.[/li]<br />[li]**Known for:** Shimozawa is a small but important fishing village known for its icefish, a rare delicacy found only in the icy waters of the Sea of Mirrors. The villagers live a harsh existence, constantly battling the frigid conditions, but they take pride in their craft. It is said that eating the icefish during the Winter Festival brings good fortune for the coming year.[/li]<br />[/ul]<br /><br />9. Inahama<br /><br />[ul]<br />[li]**Coastal village, southeastern shores.**[/li]<br />[li]**Population:** 400.[/li]<br />[li]**Known for:** Inahama is a peaceful village known for its seaweed farms and pearl divers. The villagers are skilled at harvesting crimson pearls, which are said to form from the tears of sea kami. The annual Pearl Festival draws visitors from across Furenhoku to witness the ceremonial dive and the offering of the first pearl to the local sea spirit.[/li]<br />[/ul]

## Villages of Shugane

[h1]Villages of Shugane[/h1]<br /><br />These small villages are scattered across the main island of Shugane, each with its own unique identity, local legends, and specialties. Though small in size, these settlements contribute to the rich cultural and spiritual landscape of the region. Below is a brief overview of the notable villages in Shugane, highlighting their location, population, and what they are known for.<br /><br />[h2]1. Shimizu[/h2]<br />[ul]<br />[li]**Mountain village, northern mountains.**[/li]<br />[li]**Population:** 500.[/li]<br />[li]**Known for:** A famous hermit-swordsmith named *Hoshinobu* is said to have lived in the caves above the village. His legendary blade, the *“Storm’s Edge,”* is rumored to be hidden somewhere in the mountains.[/li]<br />[/ul]<br /><br />[h2]2. Kasumi[/h2]<br />[ul]<br />[li]**Forest village, northern woods.**[/li]<br />[li]**Population:** 450.[/li]<br />[li]**Known for:** The village is shrouded in perpetual mist and is said to be haunted by the *“Mistwalker,”* a spirit that protects the villagers but curses outsiders who venture too deep.[/li]<br />[/ul]<br /><br />[h2]3. Natsubara[/h2]<br />[ul]<br />[li]**Farming village, southern plains.**[/li]<br />[li]**Population:** 900.[/li]<br />[li]**Known for:** The fertile plains of Natsubara are renowned for producing the finest rice in all of Shugane. It is believed that the fields are blessed by the Kami of Abundance.[/li]<br />[/ul]<br /><br />[h2]4. Takeshi[/h2]<br />[ul]<br />[li]**Fishing village, eastern coast.**[/li]<br />[li]**Population:** 500.[/li]<br />[li]**Known for:** Takeshi’s fishermen are famous for their skill in capturing the elusive *“Silverfin,”* a rare fish said to bring good fortune if eaten during the New Year festival.[/li]<br />[/ul]<br /><br />[h2]5. Yamabuki[/h2]<br />[ul]<br />[li]**Forest village, central forest.**[/li]<br />[li]**Population:** 400.[/li]<br />[li]**Known for:** Yamabuki is known for its wild herbs and medicinal plants, harvested by the village’s skilled herbalists. Minamito[/h2]<br />[ul]<br />[li]**Farming village, southern coast.**[/li]<br />[li]**Population:** 750.[/li]<br />[li]**Known for:** The village is famous for its citrus orchards. ## Villages of Torinokai

[h1]Villages of Torinokai[/h1]<br /><br />The villages of Torinokai are scattered across the mountainous and volcanic landscape, each one nestled in the shadow of mighty peaks or along the coastlines battered by storms. These small settlements are more than just homes; they are centers of tradition, craftsmanship, and spiritual devotion. Below is an overview of the notable villages in Torinokai, highlighting their location, population, and unique qualities.<br /><br />[h2]1. Kaikyo[/h2]<br />[ul]<br />[li]**Coastal village, southwestern cliffs.**[/li]<br />[li]**Population:** 600.[/li]<br />[li]**Known for:** A haven for storm-sailors and navigators, Kaikyo is famous for its *“Stormguides,”* mystics who claim to read the winds and currents, helping ships navigate the treacherous coastal waters.[/li]<br />[/ul]<br /><br />[h2]2. Mizuho[/h2]<br />[ul]<br />[li]**River village, northern foothills.**[/li]<br />[li]**Population:** 450.[/li]<br />[li]**Known for:** Known for its crystal-clear waters and abundant fish, Mizuho is a popular pilgrimage site for those seeking purification. The village’s *“Water Festival”* is celebrated annually to honor the water kami.[/li]<br />[/ul]<br /><br />[h2]3. Sumiyoshi[/h2]<br />[ul]<br />[li]**Mountain village, central peaks.**[/li]<br />[li]**Population:** 500.[/li]<br />[li]**Known for:** Famous for its charcoal and ironworking. The blacksmiths of Sumiyoshi use the purest charcoal to forge weapons believed to be blessed by the mountain kami.[/li]<br />[/ul]<br /><br />[h2]4. Amakusa[/h2]<br />[ul]<br />[li]**Onsen village, volcanic springs.**[/li]<br />[li]**Population:** 400.[/li]<br />[li]**Known for:** Renowned for its healing hot springs, Amakusa’s inns and bathhouses are frequented by warriors seeking to rejuvenate their bodies and spirits. The village is also home to a sacred site where the *“Spirit of the Flame”* is said to appear during festivals.[/li]<br />[/ul]<br /><br />[h2]5. Kuroishi[/h2]<br />[ul]<br />[li]**Mountain village, southern slopes.**[/li]<br />[li]**Population:** 350.[/li]<br />[li]**Known for:** A remote village known for its obsidian mines. The villagers are expert stonecutters, and their dark, glass-like sculptures are prized throughout Torinokai.[/li]<br />[/ul]<br /><br />[h2]6. Akagane[/h2]<br />[ul]<br />[li]**Mining village, western highlands.**[/li]<br />[li]**Population:** 500.[/li]<br />[li]**Known for:** Akagane produces high-quality copper and bronze, used in everything from weaponry to ceremonial artifacts. It is said that a legendary bronze golem guards the deepest part of the mine, protecting the village’s wealth.[/li]<br />[/ul]<br /><br />[h2]7. Inaba[/h2]<br />[ul]<br />[li]**Farming village, eastern foothills.**[/li]<br />[li]**Population:** 700.[/li]<br />[li]**Known for:** Known for its unique *“Dragonroot”* crop, a rare plant believed to have potent magical properties. Inaba’s fields are said to be blessed by an ancient earth kami, making the soil uniquely fertile.[/li]<br />[/ul]<br /><br />[h2]8. Yamagata[/h2]<br />[ul]<br />[li]**Mountain village, northern ridges.**[/li]<br />[li]**Population:** 300.[/li]<br />[li]**Known for:** Yamagata is known for its isolation and mystical reputation. The village is built on a high ridge, and the locals are said to be guided by the spirits of the mountains. Few outsiders visit, as it is considered a place of intense spiritual power.[/li]<br />[/ul]<br /><br />[h2]9. Nishihara[/h2]<br />[ul]<br />[li]**Fishing village, northwestern coast.**[/li]<br />[li]**Population:** 450.[/li]<br />[li]**Known for:** Nishihara’s fishermen are famous for their skill in hunting *“Firefish,”* a rare species that is believed to channel the energy of the volcano. The village’s festival, the *“Dance of the Fiery Scales,”* is celebrated each summer.[/li]<br />[/ul]<br /><br />[h2]10. Kamiyama[/h2]<br />[ul]<br />[li]**Forest village, southern woods.**[/li]<br />[li]**Population:** 500.[/li]<br />[li]**Known for:** Kamiyama is known for its medicinal herbs and the shrine of the *“Forest Guardian,”* a powerful spirit said to protect the village. The locals are skilled herbalists, and their remedies are sought throughout Torinokai.[/li]<br />[/ul]
