# The Well and the One Tree

## The Tale of the Well and the Verdant Veil

In the **Emerald Hills**, known as *Aokusa*, a land of natural splendor and harmony, stands the **One Tree**, called *Hitotsu Ki*, a colossal and ancient being whose roots connect the land's vitality to the spiritual realm.

Every thousand years, the people of Aokusa select a **Verdant Seeker**, or *Midori no Kenshōsha*, to embark on a perilous journey to the **Sacred Well of Yūmei**, also known as the Well of Radiance. The Seeker's mission is to retrieve a vial of its sacred water to renew the blessings that sustain *Hitotsu Ki* and the land itself.

**Now, let's delve into the Frostwood's version of the tale.** In Shuigari, the Well of Life is remembered differently. It is known as the **Verdant Veil**, or *Midori no Kegai*, a mythical spring concealed deep within the **Fuyumori**, the Frostwood.

## The Song of the Verdant Veil

The people of Shuigari share an ancient song, passed down through generations, whispered by hunters and woodcutters, about the spring:

*"Where frost and shadow embrace light,*
*Beneath the boughs, the veil shines bright.*
*Step with caution and breathe the air,*
*For life's true gift lies waiting there."*

## The Hydra of the Shifting Branches (Shintai no Jūhebi)

**In this version, the Hydra of the Shifting Branches, also known as Shintai no Jūhebi, emerges as a significant element. This fearsome creature is said to inhabit the depths of the Frostwood, guarding the Verdant Veil.**

To protect the *Midori no Kegai*, a fearsome creature known as the **Hydra of the Shifting Branches**, or *Shintai no Jūhebi* ("Sacred Hydra of Branching Bodies"), guards the spring.

The hydra's numerous heads resemble the twisting boughs of the ancient trees in Fuyumori, its bark-like scales blending seamlessly into the forest. It is said that severing one of the hydra's heads only results in two more growing in its place, each more formidable than the last. Some tales claim that the hydra itself is a manifestation of the forest's will, fiercely defending its most sacred places from intrusion.

## The Seekers Who Came Before

The people of Shuigari speak of rare visitors—strangers with a clear purpose and reverence in their hearts—who sought the spring. These wanderers bear a striking resemblance to the descriptions of past Verdant Seekers:

1. **Kaida Iwae** (*Kaida Stonebark*):
   A towering woman whose stone-like armor seemed to be an integral part of her being. She is remembered in Shuigari as *Kaida Iwae* ("Kaida of the Rock Tribute"), a formidable warrior who valiantly fought the *Jūhebi* to reach the sacred spring.

2. **Merrik Kagekusa** (*Merrik Greenshadow*):
   A cloaked figure who moved with the grace of a shadow through the Frostwood. Known in Shuigari as *Merrik Kagekusa* ("Merrik of the Shadowed Grass"), he was said to vanish into the depths of the forest, never to return.

## Connections Between the Two Lands

Thornbringer's discovery of the Frostwood's version of the tale creates an intriguing connection between his homeland and Shuigari. It suggests that the **Sacred Well of Yūmei** and the **Midori no Kegai** may be two facets of a profound ancient spiritual phenomenon, connected by ley lines and the delicate balance of life.

## The Legend of Fuyumori's Veil

*As told by a wandering bard in the hearth of a mountain inn, the firelight flickering with every word.*

"Long before the Shogunate rose, when the Frostwood was wild and untamed, there lived a young hunter named **Rensuke of the Silent Step**. He was a man of humble birth but unmatched skill, known to track even the faintest trail in snow so deep it swallowed lesser hunters whole. His bow sang true, his steps left no mark, and his heart was unyielding as the winter winds.

But even the boldest hearts falter in the face of the unknown.

One harsh winter, Rensuke's village fell to famine. The Frostwood, once bountiful, grew quiet and empty, as if the spirits themselves had retreated into the shadows. Elders whispered of the **Midori no Kegai**, the Verdant Veil, a spring hidden deep in the forest, said to bless the land with life and abundance. Yet none dared seek it, for the woods twisted their paths, and its guardian, the **Shintai no Jūhebi**, the Hydra of the Shifting Branches, guarded the veil with ferocity unmatched.

Rensuke, driven by desperation and duty to his kin, swore to find the Veil. Armed with little more than his bow and his resolve, he ventured into the Frostwood. Days turned to weeks as the forest tested his will. Snowstorms rose from clear skies, and trees bent unnaturally, blocking his path. Yet Rensuke pressed on, guided by faint whispers on the wind.

On the eve of his seventh week, as the moons of Intara and Dendaro crested the horizon, he found himself in a glade like no other. The trees there were ancient, their branches intertwining to form a living cathedral. At the center was the Veil—a pool of water so clear it reflected the heavens as if no earth surrounded it. But as Rensuke approached, the ground shook.

From the shadow of the trees emerged the Hydra, its heads weaving like serpents through the air, each crowned with twisting antlers. Its bark-like scales glistened with frost, and its breath carried the scent of decay. The guardian spoke, its voice echoing as though it were the forest itself:

*'Mortal, you tread upon hallowed ground. Life flows here, but it does not belong to you. Turn back, or face the will of the woods.'*

But Rensuke did not falter. Drawing his bow, he loosed an arrow tipped with the only treasure his family had left—a shard of star iron. The arrow struck true, severing one of the Hydra's heads, but where one fell, two more rose, their eyes burning with fury. The battle raged, Rensuke darting between trees, his arrows finding their marks but never tipping the scales in his favor.

As dawn broke, Rensuke stood battered and bloodied, his quiver empty. The Hydra loomed over him, its heads ready to strike, when he fell to his knees. Instead of begging for mercy, he spoke.

*'I sought the Veil not for myself but for my people, who suffer without its blessing. If my life is the cost, so be it. But spare them, O guardian, and let this forest thrive once more.'*

For the first time, the Hydra paused. The winds stilled, and the forest seemed to listen. The creature, moved by Rensuke's sacrifice, lowered its heads. Without a word, it receded into the shadows, leaving the hunter alone in the glade.

Rensuke approached the Veil, his reflection rippling in its waters. He filled his flask and returned to his village, where the water healed their ailing crops and brought life back to the land. But Rensuke, weary and worn, vanished soon after, some say to return to the forest, others to walk among the stars.

To this day, hunters and travelers whisper of his courage, and the Veil remains hidden, waiting for the next soul bold enough—or desperate enough—to seek its gift. But beware, they say, for the Hydra still watches, its many heads ready to judge the worth of those who dare to tread its domain."
