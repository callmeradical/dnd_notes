# Currency and Trade Systems of Shuigari

## Overview

Shuigari's economy operates on a dual-value system combining rice-based tribute with metallic currency. The tension between traditional rice measurement and the rising importance of coined money reflects the broader conflict between tradition and innovation across the islands.

## Standard Currency

### The Koku System (Rice Standard)
The traditional measure of wealth and taxation throughout Shuigari.

**Koku (石)**
- One koku = approximately 150 kg of rice (enough to feed one person for one year)
- Used to measure:
  - Daimyo wealth and domain size
  - Samurai stipends
  - Tax obligations
  - Land value
- A minor daimyo controls 10,000+ koku
- A major daimyo controls 100,000+ koku
- The Shogun's direct holdings exceed 4,000,000 koku

### Coined Money

**Ryo (両) - Gold Standard**
- Large gold oval coins stamped with the Shogun's mon (family crest)
- Primary currency for major transactions
- 1 ryo = approximately 1 koku of rice in value (though this fluctuates)
- Used for:
  - Land purchases
  - Large-scale merchant deals
  - Daimyo-to-daimyo transactions
  - Hiring master craftsmen
  - Enchanted weapon purchases

**Bu (分) - Silver Standard**
- Rectangular silver coins
- 4 bu = 1 ryo
- Used for:
  - Fine goods purchases
  - Artisan commissions
  - Inn stays (quality establishments)
  - Tool and weapon purchases
  - Monthly stipends for lesser samurai

**Shu (朱) - Gold/Silver Alloy**
- Small rectangular coins
- 4 shu = 1 bu (16 shu = 1 ryo)
- Used for:
  - Weekly provisions
  - Common merchandise
  - Sake and entertainment
  - Basic inn lodging
  - Everyday samurai expenses

**Mon (文) - Copper/Bronze**
- Round coins with square hole (can be strung together)
- 1,000 mon = 1 shu (approximately)
- Used for:
  - Daily food purchases
  - Street vendor goods
  - Basic services
  - Peasant/commoner transactions
  - Temple offerings

### Quick Reference Exchange Rates

```
1 Ryo = 4 Bu = 16 Shu = 16,000 Mon
1 Bu = 4 Shu = 4,000 Mon  
1 Shu = 1,000 Mon
```

## Regional Variations

### Takashima Prefecture
- **Iron Mon**: Special copper coins alloyed with small amounts of iron, minted in Takashima
- Slightly more valuable than standard mon due to iron content
- Accepted at 1.1:1 ratio in other prefectures
- Symbol of Takashima's metallurgical dominance

### Hikosan (Capital)
- **Imperial Ryo**: Gold ryo stamped with both Shogun and Emperor's seals
- Used only in capital for official Shogunate business
- Valued at 1.2 standard ryo due to political significance
- Counterfeiting carries death penalty

### Harado (Merchant Hub)
- **Trade Chits**: Wooden or bamboo tokens issued by major merchant guilds
- Redeemable for goods/services within guild networks
- Allow merchants to avoid carrying heavy coin on long journeys
- 1 chit = 1 ryo equivalent (backed by guild treasuries)

### Port Cities
- **Harbor Notes**: Paper certificates backed by merchant consortiums
- Used for large ship cargo transactions
- Denominations: 10, 50, 100, 500 ryo
- Must be redeemed within 90 days or lose 10% value

## Foreign Currency

### Mainland Currencies (Pre-Sundering Legacy)

**Dragon Marks (Ryugin 竜銀)**
- Silver coins from the collapsed mainland empire
- No longer minted but still circulate
- Collectors and scholars prize them
- 1 Dragon Mark = 3 bu (historical prestige value)
- Often melted down for silver content

**Western Trade Coins**
- Occasional foreign merchants bring coins from distant lands across the Sea of Mirrors
- Accepted only at major ports
- Must be assayed and exchanged for Shuigari currency
- Exchange rate varies wildly (3:1 to 6:1 ratio depending on purity and port master's mood)

## Taxation System

### The Rice Tax (Nengu 年貢)
Primary form of taxation throughout Shuigari.

**Standard Rate**
- Peasants owe 40-60% of rice harvest to their lord
- Rate varies by prefecture and harvest quality
- Collected twice yearly (after main and secondary harvests)

**Collection Process**
1. Village headman measures total harvest
2. Lord's tax collector verifies and records
3. Rice transported to daimyo's storehouse
4. Portion forwarded to Shogunate as tribute
5. Remainder used to:
   - Pay samurai stipends in rice
   - Maintain military reserves
   - Sell for coined money at market

**Special Levies**
- War Tax: Additional 10-20% during military campaigns
- Disaster Relief: May be reduced or suspended after famines/earthquakes
- Infrastructure Tax: Extra rice for road/bridge/irrigation projects

### Monetary Taxation

**Merchant Taxes**
- 10% of gross trade value (paid quarterly)
- Major guilds pay directly to Shogunate
- Small merchants pay through local magistrates
- Luxury goods taxed at 15%

**Craft Taxes**
- Master artisans pay annual fee: 5 ryo
- Journeymen pay annual fee: 1 ryo  
- Apprentices exempt
- Weapon smiths pay double (military importance)

**Port Duties**
- Import/Export: 5% of cargo value
- Docking fees: 1 bu per day for merchant vessels
- Naval inspection: 2 shu per foreign ship

**Mining Taxes**
- 30% of ore extracted goes to domain lord
- Additional 10% to Shogunate for strategic metals (iron, steel)
- Takashima's iron mines generate massive revenue through this system

## Economic Classes and Typical Wealth

### Peasant Farmers
- Annual Income: 1-3 koku (subsistence)
- Cash on hand: 500-2,000 mon
- Lives entirely in mon economy
- Rarely sees silver or gold

### Artisans/Merchants
- Annual Income: 5-30 koku equivalent
- Cash on hand: 2-10 bu
- Daily transactions in mon and shu
- Larger deals in bu and ryo

### Samurai (Lower Ranks)
- Annual Stipend: 50-200 koku
- Paid in rice, converts some to coin
- Regular expenses: 3-6 shu per month
- Debts common (living above means)

### Samurai (High Ranks)
- Annual Stipend: 500-3,000 koku
- Receives mix of rice and coin
- Monthly expenses: 5-20 ryo
- Maintains household, servants, weapons

### Daimyo (Minor)
- Domain Revenue: 10,000-50,000 koku
- Must pay:
  - Samurai retainers (30-40% of revenue)
  - Shogunate tribute (10-15%)
  - Infrastructure/military (20-30%)
- Personal wealth: 100-500 ryo liquid

### Daimyo (Major)
- Domain Revenue: 100,000-500,000 koku
- Similar percentage obligations but higher absolute numbers
- Personal wealth: 1,000-10,000 ryo liquid
- Can influence Shogunate policy through loans

### Merchant Guild Masters
- Annual Profits: 50,000-200,000 koku equivalent
- Liquid Assets: 5,000-50,000 ryo
- Politically powerless but economically vital
- Secret loans to daimyo create leverage

## Prices and Cost of Living

### Food (Daily)
- Bowl of rice: 5 mon
- Fish/vegetables: 10-20 mon
- Sake (cheap): 15 mon per flask
- Sake (quality): 1 shu per flask
- Full meal (common inn): 30-50 mon
- Full meal (quality inn): 2-4 shu
- Banquet (per person): 1-3 bu

### Lodging
- Common inn: 100 mon per night
- Quality inn: 1 shu per night  
- High-class inn: 2-4 shu per night
- Daimyo suite: 1+ bu per night

### Clothing
- Peasant work clothes: 200-500 mon
- Merchant kimono: 1-3 shu
- Samurai formal wear: 2-5 bu
- Silk kimono (fine): 1-3 ryo
- Master artisan commission: 5-20 ryo

### Weapons & Armor
- Simple iron sword: 2-4 shu
- Quality katana: 3-8 bu
- Master-forged katana: 5-20 ryo
- Legendary blade: 100+ ryo (plus favors/quests)
- Full armor (ashigaru): 1-2 bu
- Full armor (samurai): 5-15 bu
- Ceremonial armor: 3-10 ryo

### Services
- Healer (common ailment): 50-200 mon
- Doctor (surgery): 1-5 shu
- Scribe (letter): 20 mon
- Scribe (contract): 1-3 shu
- Fortune teller: 50-500 mon
- Priest blessing: 100-1,000 mon
- Onmyoji ritual: 1 bu - 5 ryo (depending on complexity)

### Animals & Transport
- Draft horse: 5-10 bu
- War horse: 3-8 ryo
- Ox: 2-4 bu
- Palanquin rental: 500 mon per day
- Cart rental: 200 mon per day
- River ferry: 10-50 mon
- Coastal passage: 1-5 shu

### Tools & Equipment
- Farming tools (set): 500-1,000 mon
- Carpentry tools: 1-3 shu
- Smithing tools (basic): 1-2 bu
- Masterwork tools: 5-15 bu
- Enchanting supplies: 2-10 ryo

## Economic Challenges

### Inflation
- Prolonged warfare drains Shogunate treasury
- More coin minted with less gold/silver content
- Trust in currency declining
- Some merchants demand payment in pure metal or rice

### Counterfeiting
- Rising problem in major cities
- Criminal organizations (Emerald Lotus Syndicate) profit hugely
- Penalties severe: death by crucifixion
- Detection methods:
  - Weight testing
  - Sound testing (experts can hear fake coins)
  - Seal inspection
  - Merchant guild stamps

### Debt Culture
- Samurai commonly in debt to merchants
- Code of honor prevents open commerce
- Secret arrangements through intermediaries
- Some samurai become ronin to escape debts
- Daimyo accumulate massive loans to fund military

### Regional Imbalances
- Takashima's iron wealth vs. agricultural regions
- Port cities accumulating coin, rural areas rice-poor
- Merchant class growing wealthier than lower samurai
- Threatens traditional social order

## Money-Lending and Banking

### Merchant Guilds
- Function as unofficial banks
- Lend to daimyo at 10-20% annual interest
- Foreclose impossible (social class barriers)
- Power through accumulation of uncollectable IOUs

### Temple Banks
- Buddhist temples provide small loans to commoners
- 5-15% interest (lower than guilds)
- Collateral: land, tools, family heirlooms
- More ethical but limited capital

### Pawnbrokers
- Accept any collateral
- 20-50% interest on short-term loans
- Frequent customers: lower samurai, gambling addicts
- Often fronts for criminal organizations

## Black Market Economics

### Shadow Market Rates
- Stolen goods: 30-50% of legitimate value
- Forbidden items: 200-500% markup
- Smuggled foreign goods: 150-300% markup
- Information: 1 shu - 10 ryo depending on value

### Grey Tide Economy
- Pirate gold (looted): Melted and recast
- Contraband weapons: 150% normal price
- Enslaved workers: 5-20 ryo per person (abhorrent but occurs)
- Protection rackets: 10-30% of merchant profits

### Obsidian Circle Resources
- Massive hidden wealth (source unknown)
- Can outbid legitimate buyers for artifacts
- Pays premium for information: 3-5x normal rates
- Recruiting bonuses: 10 ryo signing payment for skilled warriors
- Operating budget suggests funding from powerful backer

## Campaign Integration

### Plot Hooks
- PCs discover counterfeit ring
- Daimyo's debt to merchant guild threatens domain
- Shadow Market dealing in cursed artifacts
- Rice tax creates peasant uprising
- Foreign currency appearing in volume (trade route opening?)

### Economic Warfare
- Obsidian Circle manipulating rice markets
- Daimyo bankrupting rival through trade manipulation
- Merchant guild threatening to call in Shogunate's loans
- Inflation causing social unrest

- 50 ryo is life-changing money
- 500 ryo could buy a small estate
- 5,000 ryo could fund a minor rebellion
