# Basilisk

## Basilisk
*Medium monstrosity, unaligned*

**Armor Class** 15 (Natural Armor)  
**Hit Points** 52 (8d8+16)  
**Speed** 20 ft.

| STR | DEX | CON | INT | WIS | CHA |
|-----|-----|-----|-----|-----|-----|
| 16(+3) | 8(-1) | 15(+2) | 2(-4) | 8(-1) | 7(-2) |

**Senses** darkvision 60 ft., passive Perception 9  
**Languages** —  
**Challenge** 3 (700 XP)  
**Proficiency Bonus** +2

### Features

**Petrifying Gaze.** If a creature starts its turn within 30 feet of the basilisk and the two of them can see each other, the basilisk can force the creature to make a DC 12 Constitution saving throw if the basilisk isn't incapacitated. On a failed save, the creature magically begins to turn to stone and is restrained. It must repeat the saving throw at the end of its next turn. On a success, the effect ends. On a failure, the creature is petrified until freed by the greater restoration spell or other magic.

A creature that isn't surprised can avert its eyes to avoid the saving throw at the start of its turn. If it does so, it can't see the basilisk until the start of its next turn, when it can avert its eyes again. If it looks at the basilisk in the meantime, it must immediately make the save.

If the basilisk sees its reflection within 30 feet of it in bright light, it mistakes itself for a rival and targets itself with its gaze.

### Actions

**Bite.** *Melee Weapon Attack:* +5 to hit, reach 5 ft., one target. *Hit:* 10 (2d6+3) piercing damage plus 7 (2d6) poison damage.

---

### Encounter Information

**Number Appearing:** 1 basilisk (always solitary)  
**Environment:** Rocky caves, vine-covered ruins, stone quarries  
**Encounter Trigger:** Random wildlife encounter (#8 on d8)

### Roleplay Information

**Personality Traits**

- Territorial creature that views all intruders as threats to be eliminated
- Patient ambush predator that uses camouflage and surprise
- Instinctively hostile to other basilisks and creatures that meet its gaze

**Mannerisms**

- Moves with slow, deliberate reptilian grace
- Eyes glow with malevolent yellow light when using petrifying gaze
- Creates soft scraping sounds when moving across stone surfaces
- Freezes motionless when hunting, blending with rocky surroundings

**Tactics**

- Positions itself among stone statues (previous victims) for camouflage
- Uses petrifying gaze as primary weapon, bite as backup
- Attempts to catch enemies by surprise when they discover petrified victims
- Fights from defensive positions where it can control line of sight
- Never retreats from its territory, views all encounters as life-or-death

**Environment Notes**

- Lair surrounded by petrified remains of previous victims
- Stone statues in various poses of terror mark basilisk territory
- Plants and small animals in area show signs of petrification
- Rocky areas chosen for defensibility and camouflage opportunities

## GM Notes

### Running This Encounter

**Pre-Encounter Setup**
- Prepare 2d4+1 petrified victim statues around basilisk's lair
- Determine basilisk's hiding position among the stone figures
- Note lighting conditions: bright light allows mirror trick with shields/water
- Consider party's knowledge of basilisk abilities and countermeasures

**Opening Scene Options**

**Garden of Stone:**
> *The vine-covered ruins ahead contain an unusual sight—stone statues scattered throughout the crumbling courtyard. As you draw closer, the craftsmanship seems remarkably lifelike, capturing expressions of surprise and terror in perfect detail. Then you realize these aren't statues at all, but the petrified remains of previous travelers.*

**Ambush from the Stones:**
> *Among the stone figures, something moves. What you took for another petrified victim slowly turns its reptilian head toward your group. The basilisk's yellow eyes begin to glow with malevolent power as it fixes its deadly gaze upon you.*

**Encounter Phases**

**Phase 1: The Gaze (Immediate Threat)**
- Basilisk uses petrifying gaze on party members who can see it
- Party must decide whether to avert eyes (combat disadvantage) or risk petrification
- Basilisk positions itself to maximize gaze effectiveness
- **Constitution Save (DC 12):** Resist petrification on first exposure
- **Perception Check (DC 15):** Notice basilisk among petrified victims before gaze activates

**Phase 2: Tactical Positioning**
- Basilisk uses terrain and stone statues for cover
- Party must navigate around obstacles while avoiding direct eye contact
- Creative solutions like mirrors or indirect viewing become crucial
- **Constitution Save (DC 12):** Required each turn if looking at basilisk
- **Survival Check (DC 13):** Navigate terrain effectively while averting eyes

**Phase 3: Desperate Measures**
- If party members begin turning to stone, encounter becomes race against time
- Basilisk becomes more aggressive, closing for bite attacks
- Mirror trick or creative solutions may turn tide of battle
- **Medicine Check (DC 15):** Stabilize partially petrified party members

**Petrification Mechanics**

**Two-Stage Process**
- First failed save: Restrained condition, get to try again next turn
- Second failed save: Full petrification, requires greater restoration
- Success on second save: Effect ends, return to normal

**Averting Eyes Strategy**
- Prevents automatic saving throws
- Causes disadvantage on attack rolls against basilisk
- Can still make attacks but with reduced effectiveness
- Must declare at start of turn, lasts until next turn

**Mirror Counter-Strategy**
- Polished shields, water surfaces, or mirrors allow indirect viewing
- Requires bright light to work effectively
- Basilisk will target itself if it sees reflection
- Creative problem-solving opportunity for players

**Environmental Combat**

**Terrain Usage**
- Stone statues provide cover for both sides
- Ruins and rocky areas create complex battlefield
- Basilisk knows terrain intimately and uses it tactically
- Difficult terrain may slow party movement

**Lighting Considerations**
- Bright light enables mirror strategies
- Dim light makes indirect viewing more difficult
- Darkness negates gaze attack but also party vision
- Torches and magical light become tactical resources

**Skill Integration**
- **Arcana (DC 14):** Understand petrification magic and potential counters
- **Investigation (DC 12):** Examine petrified victims for clues about basilisk tactics
- **Medicine (DC 15):** Recognize stages of petrification and treatment options
- **Nature (DC 13):** Know basilisk behavioral patterns and weaknesses

**Creative Solutions**

**Indirect Attack Methods**
- **Mirrors and Reflective Surfaces:** Turn gaze against basilisk
- **Blind Fighting:** Close eyes completely and use other senses
- **Area Effect Spells:** Target basilisk's location without direct sight
- **Summoned Creatures:** Use expendable allies to distract basilisk

**Environmental Exploitation**
- **Bright Light Sources:** Enable mirror strategies
- **Water Features:** Create improvised reflecting surfaces
- **Smoke/Fog:** Block line of sight for gaze attacks
- **Rubble/Debris:** Thrown objects don't require direct sight

**Aftermath Considerations**

**If Basilisk Defeated**
- Petrified victims may be restored with appropriate magic
- Basilisk hide valuable for crafting magic resistance items
- Eyes retain some petrification magic for alchemical use
- Lair may contain treasure from previous victims

**If Party Members Petrified**
- Greater restoration or similar magic required for cure
- Stone-to-flesh scrolls or potions might be available
- Adds urgency to find magical healing
- Creates memorable consequences for tactical failures

**Treasure and Recovery**
- 3d6 × 10 gp in coins and gems from petrified victims
- 1d3 magic items that survived petrification process
- Basilisk parts worth 200-400 gp to alchemists and enchanters
- Possible scrolls or potions in lair

**Story Integration Hooks**
- Missing persons reports lead party to basilisk's hunting ground
- Important NPC petrified, party must find cure
- Basilisk guards access to ancient ruins or treasure vault
- Local legend about "garden of stone" proves terrifyingly real

**Scaling Options**
- **Easier:** Reduce save DC to 10, give warning signs before encounter
- **Harder:** Multiple basilisks (extremely rare) or enhanced poison bite
- **Environmental:** Combat in total darkness (negates gaze) or with environmental hazards
- **Tactical:** Basilisk has learned to use mirrors against enemies
