# Scientific and Technical Knowledge in Shuigari

## Overview

While Shuigari's civilization is steeped in spiritual tradition and mystical practice, practical technical knowledge has developed alongside the supernatural. The boundary between "science" and "magic" is often blurred—a master smith understands metallurgy both as chemical process and spiritual communion with fire kami. Knowledge is preserved in specialized texts, oral traditions, and guild secrets, creating a complex landscape of expertise.

## Metallurgy and Materials Science

### Theoretical Understanding

**Metal Properties (Kinzoku no Seishitsu 金属の性質)**

*The Five Metal Essences:*
Traditional metallurgical theory organizes metals by spiritual and physical properties:

- **Iron (Tetsu 鉄)**: Element of strength and endurance
  - Hardens when heated and quenched
  - "Remembers" treatment (work hardening understood empirically)
  - Rusts (explained as "return to earth")
  - Magnetic properties (spiritual attraction to earth kami)

- **Steel (Hagane 鋼)**: Refined iron with "captured fire"
  - Created through precise carbon addition
  - Folding purifies and strengthens
  - Different carbon levels create different properties
  - Spring steel vs. tool steel vs. sword steel distinguished

- **Copper (Dō 銅)**: Element of conduction and change
  - Conducts heat and magical energy
  - Alloys easily (spiritual compatibility with other metals)
  - Greenish corrosion (copper's spiritual color emerging)

- **Bronze (Seidō 青銅)**: Marriage of copper and tin
  - Ancient metal (historical significance)
  - Bells and sacred vessels (spiritual resonance)
  - Ratio of tin to copper creates different properties

- **Gold/Silver (Kin/Gin 金/銀)**: Pure metals, incorruptible
  - Never tarnish (spiritual permanence)
  - Precious but soft (nobility without strength)
  - Used in ritual objects (connection to kami)

**Practical Metallurgy Texts:**

*"The Thousand Folds" (Senkai 千回)*
- Master work on sword-making
- Describes folding techniques
- Temperature reading by color (visual pyrometry)
- Quenching methods and water additives
- Exists in multiple versions (guild secrets omitted)
- Available version: 8 ryo, Complete guild version: Restricted

*"Harmony of Ore and Fire" (Kōka no Chōwa 鉱火の調和)*
- Smelting processes
- Ore identification and quality
- Flux materials and their effects
- Furnace design and operation
- Written 200 years ago, still authoritative
- Cost: 5 ryo

*"Secret Traditions of the Mountain Forge"*
- Takashima master smiths' manual
- Temperature control techniques
- Alloy recipes
- Tool steel production
- Guild members only, death penalty for revealing

### Practical Techniques

**Temperature Control:**
- Color-based pyrometry (precise to ~50°C)
- Orange-red: ~900°C (forging temperature)
- Cherry red: ~700°C (tempering)
- White heat: ~1300°C (welding temperature)
- Master smiths can distinguish shades others miss

**Quenching Media:**
- Water: Fast cooling, harder but more brittle
- Oil: Slower cooling, tougher
- Brine: Faster than water, risks cracking
- Special recipes: Water with clay, vinegar, sake
- Theory: Different media have different kami that influence cooling

**Pattern Welding:**
- Layering different steel types
- Creates distinctive patterns
- Functional (combines hard/tough steels)
- Aesthetic (beautiful grain patterns)
- Some patterns considered spiritually significant

**Surface Treatments:**
- Lacquering (rust prevention)
- Chemical patination (blue-black finish)
- Polishing techniques (different grades)
- Theory vs. practice both taught

### Quality Testing

**Physical Tests:**
- Bend test (flexibility without breaking)
- Edge test (cutting various materials)
- Endurance test (repeated striking)
- Visual inspection (grain structure)

**Spiritual Tests:**
- Water test (how water moves on surface)
- Sound test (ring when struck)
- "Feel" (intuitive assessment)
- Divination (onmyoji consultation for important blades)

**Standardization:**
- Major guilds have quality grades
- Testing procedures written in manuals
- Stamps indicate quality level
- Reputation depends on consistent quality

## Medicine and Anatomy

### Medical Theory

**The Four Humors of Shuigari:**

Based on imported continental knowledge combined with local spiritual beliefs:

- **Blood (Chi 血)**: Carries life force and heat
  - Too much: Fever, aggression
  - Too little: Weakness, coldness
  - Treatment: Bleeding (reduce), tonics (increase)

- **Breath (Ki 気)**: Spiritual energy/life force
  - Flows through meridian channels
  - Blockages cause pain and illness
  - Treatment: Acupuncture, herbs, meditation

- **Water (Sui 水)**: Fluids and moisture
  - Too much: Swelling, phlegm
  - Too little: Dryness, fever
  - Treatment: Diuretics, hydration

- **Essence (Seisui 精髄)**: Core vitality
  - Depleted by age, illness, excess
  - Restored by rest, proper food, spiritual practice
  - Cannot be quickly replenished

**Medical Texts:**

*"Canon of Internal Medicine" (Naikyo 内経)*
- Translated from mainland 400 years ago
- Theory of meridians and ki flow
- Diagnostic techniques
- Herbal formulae
- Standard text for all physicians
- Cost: 10 ryo (legitimate copy)

*"Thousand Golden Remedies" (Senkin-ho 千金方)*
- Practical treatment manual
- Organized by symptoms
- Over 1,000 herbal recipes
- Acupuncture point diagrams
- Surgery techniques (limited)
- Cost: 8 ryo

*"Illustrated Guide to Medicinal Plants"*
- Identifies herbs by region and season
- Preparation methods
- Dosages
- Contraindications
- Beautiful botanical illustrations
- Cost: 12 ryo

### Practical Medicine

**Diagnostic Techniques:**

*Pulse Reading (Myakushin 脈診)*
- 12 pulse positions on wrists
- Each corresponds to organ/meridian
- Depth, rate, strength, quality assessed
- Takes years to master
- More art than science but effective

*Tongue Diagnosis*
- Color, coating, shape
- Indicates internal imbalances
- Quick and non-invasive

*Urine and Stool Examination*
- Color, consistency, smell
- Diagnostic value recognized

*Patient History*
- Detailed questioning
- Lifestyle, diet, symptoms
- Family history considered

**Treatment Methods:**

*Herbal Medicine (Kampo 漢方)*
- Multi-herb formulations
- Tailored to individual patient
- Theory: Synergistic effects
- Decoctions (boiled), powders, pills
- Preparation technique matters

*Acupuncture (Hari 鍼)*
- Metal needles (gold, silver, steel)
- Inserted at specific points
- Manipulated to move ki
- Pain relief, treat various conditions
- Requires extensive training

*Moxibustion (Okyu お灸)*
- Burning moxa (mugwort) on skin
- Warms meridians
- Treats cold/damp conditions
- Common home treatment

*Surgery (Geka 外科)*
- Limited but practiced
- Wound treatment and suturing
- Boil lancing
- Amputation (last resort)
- Battlefield medicine more advanced
- Infection understood empirically (clean wounds heal better)

### Anatomical Knowledge

**Understanding of Internal Organs:**
- Based on animal dissection and battlefield observation
- Heart: Pump for blood (understood)
- Lungs: Bring in breath/ki
- Stomach/Intestines: Transform food to ki
- Liver/Kidneys: Filter blood, produce essence
- Brain: Seat of thought (debated—some say heart)

**Limitations:**
- Human dissection forbidden (religious taboo)
- Knowledge from external observation only
- Some anatomical errors but generally functional understanding

**Medical Texts:**
- Anatomical drawings (artistic but somewhat inaccurate)
- Based on animal dissection extrapolated to humans
- Battlefield surgeons have best practical knowledge

### Pharmaceutical Knowledge

**Herb Identification:**
- Over 2,000 medicinal plants catalogued
- Regional variations documented
- Seasonal collection times specified
- Parts used (root, leaf, flower, etc.)

**Preparation:**
- Drying and storage methods
- Decoction techniques (temperature, duration)
- Extraction in alcohol
- Powder grinding
- Pill binding agents

**Dosage:**
- Weight-based (patient size)
- Age considerations
- Condition severity
- Combination effects

**Toxicity:**
- Poisonous plants well documented
- Dose makes the poison (recognized)
- Antidotes known for common poisons
- Some medicines toxic in large amounts

### Public Health

**Sanitation:**
- Wells away from latrines (disease connection understood)
- Waste removal systems in cities
- Clean water importance recognized
- Quarantine of infected patients

**Epidemic Response:**
- Isolation of sick
- Burning of contaminated items
- Travel restrictions
- Theory: "Evil vapors" spread disease
- Effective even if theory wrong

## Architecture and Engineering

### Structural Principles

**Earthquake Engineering:**

Shuigari's position means earthquakes are constant threat. Builders developed sophisticated techniques:

*Foundation Systems:*
- Stone piers (not solid foundation)
- Allows building to move independently of ground
- Dissipates earthquake energy

*Flexible Frameworks:*
- Wood post-and-beam construction
- Joints use mortise-and-tenon (no nails)
- Slight play in joints allows movement
- "Building breathes" during earthquake

*Weight Distribution:*
- Heavy roof helps stability (counterintuitive but works)
- Center of gravity calculation (empirical)
- Symmetrical design reduces torsion

**Technical Manuals:**

*"Principles of the Master Carpenter" (Daiku no Genten 大工の原典)*
- Structural calculations
- Load-bearing capacity
- Joint types and applications
- Tool usage
- Timber selection
- Cost: 6 ryo

*"Building in the Shaking Land"*
- Earthquake-resistant design
- Foundation techniques
- Reinforcement methods
- Case studies of buildings that survived/failed
- Takashima publication
- Cost: 8 ryo

### Practical Techniques

**Surveying:**
- Water level (precision leveling)
- Plumb line (vertical alignment)
- String and stakes (layout)
- Measuring rods (standardized lengths)
- Compass (orientation)

**Timber Selection:**
- Different woods for different purposes
- Cedar: Weather resistant, straight grain
- Pine: Structural strength
- Cypress: Moisture resistance
- Bamboo: Flexibility, scaffolding
- Seasoning times documented

**Tool Mastery:**
- Planes (finishing)
- Saws (cutting)
- Chisels (joinery)
- Adzes (shaping)
- Tool maintenance critical
- Quality tools expensive (master carpenter's set: 10-30 ryo)

## Astronomy and Timekeeping

### Celestial Knowledge

**Calendar Calculations:**

The Imperial Onmyodo Bureau maintains official calendar:

*Lunar Calendar:*
- 12 months of 29-30 days
- Intercalary months added periodically
- New year begins on new moon
- Festivals tied to lunar calendar

*Solar Terms:*
- 24 divisions of solar year
- Mark agricultural seasons
- Important for farming, fishing
- Calculated astronomically

*Eclipse Prediction:*
- Lunar eclipses predicted accurately
- Solar eclipses roughly predicted
- Based on cycles observed over centuries
- Eclipses considered omens

**Astronomical Texts:**

*"Records of Celestial Movements"*
- Star charts
- Planetary movements
- Eclipse tables
- Comet observations
- Updated continuously
- Onmyodo Bureau only

*"Agricultural Calendar" (Koyomi 暦)*
- Published annually
- Lucky/unlucky days
- Planting dates
- Festival dates
- Weather predictions
- Cost: 200-500 mon

### Timekeeping

**Incense Clocks:**
- Calibrated incense sticks burn at known rate
- Mark hours and subdivisions
- Used in temples and government offices
- Different scents mark different times

**Water Clocks:**
- Calibrated vessels
- Water drips at constant rate
- Marks hours
- Requires regular maintenance
- More accurate than incense

**Sundials:**
- Simple but effective during day
- Calibrated for latitude
- Used in gardens and courtyards

**Hour System:**
- Day divided into 12 "hours" (actually ~2 hours)
- Named for zodiac animals
- Variable length (day hours vs. night hours)
- Subdivided into "marks" (刻 koku)

## Agriculture and Animal Husbandry

### Agricultural Science

**Soil Knowledge:**

*Soil Types Classified:*
- Black soil (rich in organic matter)
- Red clay (good drainage)
- Sandy loam (easy working)
- Heavy clay (retains water)
- Each suited to different crops

*Soil Improvement:*
- Compost and manure
- Green manure (plowing under cover crops)
- Crop rotation (restores soil)
- Terracing (prevents erosion)

**Irrigation Engineering:**

*Systems:*
- Canals from rivers
- Reservoir construction
- Water wheel pumps
- Sluice gates and flow control
- Distribution networks

*Maintenance:*
- Annual canal clearing
- Dam repair
- Conflict resolution (water rights)
- Record of water flow

**Crop Selection:**

*Rice Varieties:*
- Early ripening (avoid typhoons)
- Late ripening (higher yield)
- Mountain rice (drought resistant)
- Lowland rice (high yield)
- Selection through observation

*Selective Breeding:*
- Saving seeds from best plants
- Gradual improvement over generations
- Theory: "Like produces like"
- Some understanding of heredity

### Animal Husbandry

**Selective Breeding:**
- War horses bred for size and spirit
- Draft animals for strength
- Chickens for eggs vs. meat
- Dogs for specific purposes

**Veterinary Medicine:**
- Herbal treatments
- Surgery (castration, etc.)
- Epidemic response
- Practical knowledge passed down

**Texts:**

*"Care of Horses"*
- Breeding selection
- Training methods
- Disease treatment
- Feed and housing
- Essential for samurai
- Cost: 3 ryo
