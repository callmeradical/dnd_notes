# Food and Drink of Shuigari

Shuigari’s cuisine mirrors its geography: mountain villages favor hearty fare that wards off alpine winds, coastal prefectures celebrate the day’s catch, and the capital indulges in refined dishes served alongside fragrant teas. Use the following spreads to ground tavern scenes, festival banquets, or downtime visits to the market.

## Signature Dishes

| Dish | Type | Description | Region | Cultural Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Rice Bowl (Gohan)** | Staple | Steamed rice served with pickled greens or miso broth. | Nationwide | Forms the foundation of every meal regardless of class. |
| **Takashima Iron Stew** | Hearty stew | Root vegetables, mountain herbs, and smoked meat simmered overnight. | [Takashima Prefecture](Takashima%20Prefecture.md) | Favored by miners and smiths; often shared after long forge shifts. |
| **Yuzu-Glazed Fish** | Seafood | Grilled river fish lacquered with sweet-tart yuzu glaze. | [Harado Prefecture](Harado%20Prefecture.md) | Served during harbor festivals and family gatherings among sailors. |
| **Forest Mushroom Hotpot** | Communal stew | Wild mushrooms, bamboo shoots, and tofu simmered in savory broth. | [Shugane](Shugane.md) & surrounding forests | Hunters bring ingredients as tribute to local kami before sharing the meal. |
| **Mountain Yak Dumplings** | Snack | Steamed dumplings filled with spiced yak and mountain leeks. | [Takashima Prefecture](Takashima%20Prefecture.md) | Packed by travelers crossing the Frostwood passes; sold in roadside stalls. |
| **Celestial Sunrise Porridge** | Breakfast | Millet porridge with ginger, honey, and sun-dried peaches. | Hikosan Prefecture | Served to visiting dignitaries before morning petitions at court. |
| **Crimson Berry Skewers** | Street food | Skewered berries brushed with rice syrup and toasted sesame. | Coastal markets | Sold at lantern festivals; rumored to grant good fortune in romance. |
| **Hunter’s Skewer** | Trail ration | Charred strips of boar or stag with coarse salt and pepper. | Takashima & Frostwood Outpost | Standard ration for rangers and patrols; often paired with hot sake. |

## Libations and Tonics

| Drink | Type | Description | Region | Cultural Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Green Tea (Ryokucha)** | Hot beverage | Fresh-steeped leaves with grassy aroma. | Nationwide | Poured for guests as a sign of respect and welcome. |
| **Rice Wine (Sake)** | Alcoholic | Fermented rice wine with varying sweetness. | Nationwide | Served warm in winter, chilled in summer; central to shrine festivals. |
| **Plum Wine (Umeshu)** | Alcoholic | Sweet wine made by steeping ume fruit in shochu. | [Shugane](Shugane.md) orchards | Toasted during moon-viewing parties and betrothal ceremonies. |
| **Crimson Berry Wine** | Alcoholic | Tart, ruby wine crafted from coastal berries. | [Harado Prefecture](Harado%20Prefecture.md) | Paired with seafood feasts and celebratory returns from long voyages. |
| **Yak Butter Tea** | Hot beverage | Black tea whisked with yak butter and roasted barley flour. | Takashima Highlands | Keeps patrols warm on night watches; doubles as a hardy breakfast. |
| **Moonpetal Infusion** | Herbal tisane | Brewed moonvine petals with lavender and silvergrass. | Temple of the Three Moons | Shared during vigils to invite dreams and divinations from Tsukuyomi. |
| **Sea Breeze Iced Tea** | Cold beverage | Coastal herbs, dried kelp, and citrus peel shaken over ice. | Harado docks | Popular with dockworkers seeking a caffeine-free refresher. |

Use these tables when stocking taverns, rewarding downtime, or underscoring regional identity. Swapping a stew or tea for one of these specialties immediately signals where the characters are—and whose hospitality they’ve earned.
