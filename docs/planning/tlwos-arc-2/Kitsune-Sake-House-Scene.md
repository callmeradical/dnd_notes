# The Amber Threshold — Kitsune Sake House Scene

**When to use:** After the party accepts Misaki's invitation. Picks up directly from Block 3 of the Kitsune Village Awakening scene. Misaki walks them to the sake house door.

**Tone target:** Spirited Away energy throughout — warmth, strangeness, wonder, the sense that you have walked into a place that exists slightly outside ordinary time. The scene has weight at its center but must never feel heavy at its edges. There should be laughter in this room. Players should leave feeling fed, not just informed.

**Scene structure overview:**
1. Entering the sake house
2. Being seated, drinks arrive
3. The economy of the house reveals itself (elder kitsune demonstration)
4. The exchange — player-driven, DM-responsive
5. Levity moment (young kitsune, Pip)
6. The send-off

---

## Scene 1: Entering the Sake House

*Read as Misaki pulls aside the curtain and waves them through.*

> The noise hits you first.
>
> Not loud the way a tavern brawl is loud — loud the way a room full of people who are genuinely glad to be alive is loud. Dozens of voices layered into something that is almost music, punctuated by the ring of cups and the sharp crack of someone winning something. The air is thick with smoke and warmth and the smell of fat rendered off bone, of rice wine heated until it blooms, of cedar and something floral you cannot identify.
>
> The room is low-ceilinged and amber-lit, every surface worn to a shine from long use. Round tables crowd the floor, each one surrounded by kitsune in every state of transformation — some nearly fully human, elegant in layered robes, tails folded neatly behind them. Some halfway shifted, fox-ears and human faces and hands that are almost paws. And a few who have given up the pretense entirely: small foxes perched on cushions, chins on the table edge, watching the room with gold eyes like they own the whole enterprise.
>
> There are kitsune with one tail. With three. With four. One, near the back, has five tails fanned out behind her like a screen — she is watching you with an expression that is not unfriendly, exactly, but is the kind of look you give someone who has walked through a door you did not expect them to be able to find.
>
> Then the noise stops.
>
> Not all of it. But there is a beat — just one — where the whole room turns, takes you in, decides something, and looks away. And when the sound returns, it is louder than it was before.
>
> The sake house is pleased with itself for having guests.

**[DM note: Let this beat breathe. Players will look around. Name things they ask about. A three-tail near the door is doing small illusion tricks for a table of one-tails — foxfire shapes that dissolve when touched. A very old kitsune in a corner is asleep, six tails rising and falling slowly with his breath, an untouched cup in front of him. A young one is perched on a rafter above, watching the party specifically with the focused attention of someone making a study. That is Pip — remember Pip. He matters later.]**

## Scene 2: Being Seated

*Misaki finds them a table near the center — not hidden, not exposed. A place to be seen.*

> Misaki finds you a table near the center of the room, which you realize is deliberate. You are not being put in a corner. You are being displayed, a little. The kitsune at nearby tables glance over with open curiosity rather than pretending not to look.
>
> You have barely settled when the drinks arrive. A woman with three tails and no expression sets cups in front of each of you without asking what you want. Then a ceramic flask, warm to the touch. Then she is gone.
>
> The sake is unlike anything you have had before. It does not taste like rice wine, exactly — or it does, but underneath that there is something older. Something that reminds you, faintly and inexplicably, of a smell from childhood. Not the same smell for each of you. Something personal. It is warm all the way down and settles not in your stomach but somewhere behind your sternum, like a small fire banked for the night.
>
> Misaki pours herself a cup, holds it in both hands, and looks at you with the unhurried patience of someone who has nowhere better to be.
>
> "The house will feed you," she says. "It has been waiting a long time to feed someone."

**[DM note: The sake sensation is individualized — ask each player what childhood smell or memory flickers for their character. Don't answer for them. This is a quick, low-stakes invitation into character interiority that sets the tone for what the exchange requires. Yasei may feel something more than memory — the drink has a spiritual quality that someone with sensitivity might notice as a very faint resonance, like a tuning fork held near water. It is not dangerous. It is the house being alive.]**

**[Food arrives without being ordered throughout the scene — small plates of things that are always exactly what you wanted. Do not belabor this. Just mention it once or twice as background texture when a moment needs warmth.]**

## Scene 3: The Economy of the House

*Read this when the party has had a moment to settle — when there is a natural pause in their conversation, or after they have asked Misaki a question she deflects.*

> The five-tail you noticed when you walked in — the one near the back wall — rises from her table. She moves through the room the way water moves through stones, unhurried and inevitable, and she settles herself cross-legged on the cushion across from you without asking permission or offering explanation.
>
> She is very old. Not in the way humans look old, with softness and decline. Old in the way carved wood looks old — darker, harder, every grain more pronounced. Her hair is white and simply pinned. Her five tails lie arranged behind her like the robes of someone who has stopped needing to try.
>
> She sets a cup between you. Not hers. A third cup, empty.
>
> "I will give you something," she says. Her voice is like a temple bell struck softly from a distance. "And then you will give me something. Not because the house demands it. Because this is how things move here. Information carried one direction has no weight. Information exchanged — " she turns her palm up, then down — "settles."
>
> She looks at each of you in turn, and when her eyes find you, they are the color of autumn leaves over water — amber and deep and patient.
>
> "The prince has not slept in a moon's turn. His attendants speak of it in whispers, the way people speak of things they believe they are not allowed to say aloud. He wakes at the same hour each night and sits at his window until dawn, and in the morning he remembers nothing of it." She pauses. "I tell you this freely. A gift for the door." Another pause. "Now. Who are you?"
>
> It is not a question about your names.

**[DM note: Her name is Riko. She has five tails, she has been here longer than the city above, and she is not going to be rushed. "Who are you" is an invitation to begin the exchange — she wants to know what they carry, not what they are called. She will wait as long as it takes. She does not fill silences. If the players are uncertain what she is asking, Misaki can translate: "She wants to know what you are in the world. What you are trying to do. What it has cost you so far."]**

**[The first piece of information — the prince's sleeplessness — is given free. It establishes the pattern without requiring anything. Every exchange after this will cost something true. The more it costs to say, the more it returns.]**

## Scene 4: The Exchange

**This section is player-driven. Do not script it. The table below gives you four information tiers with corresponding offers. Move through them based on what players actually say, not what you planned.**

**Running principle:** The kitsune are not interrogators. They do not ask leading questions. They offer, they wait, and they respond to what is genuinely offered. If a player shares something surface-level and Riko senses it is a performance of vulnerability rather than the real thing, her tails go still and she waits. She will not call them a liar. She will simply not respond until something true is offered.

**Misaki's role in the exchange:** She is warmer than Riko, more willing to prompt. If the exchange stalls, she might say: "The house is not cruel. It does not ask for your worst wound. It asks only for something that is real." Or: "You have been carrying something since before you came underground. We can feel it. You do not have to name it. But you have to mean it."

---

### What the Kitsune Know — Four Tiers

#### Tier 1: Something Small
*A surface-level share — what you're doing here, what you're looking for, something anyone might say.*

**What the kitsune offer in return:**
The prince has not only lost sleep. He has been making small agreements — signing documents, approving appointments, granting permissions — that each, individually, seem reasonable. A new court advisor. A change in guard rotation. A sealed account for "festival expenses." No single act is alarming. Taken together, they add up to something that looks very much like someone patiently removing every obstacle from a path. Riko does not know where the path leads. She knows the shape of the clearing.

**Delivery line (Riko):** "The ones who use influence carefully never ask for the large thing first. They ask for ten small things. By the time the large thing comes, the habit of yes is already built."

---

#### Tier 2: A Genuine Vulnerability or Fear
*Something that costs something to say. Not performance. Not tactical disclosure. Something real.*

**What the kitsune offer in return:**
The Oni Mage works through reflection. Not illusion — reflection. He shows people a version of themselves they desperately wish were true: wiser, stronger, more decisive, more worthy. The first lie is never forced, the sage said. But the reflection is more seductive than force ever could be. The prince sees himself as a great man in the Oni's company. This is the hook, and it is not breakable from outside. No one can be argued out of a self they have chosen. The reflection has to be cracked from within.

**How to crack it:** The Oni Mage cannot maintain the reflection in the presence of genuine grief. Not performed grief. Real grief — the kind that does not know it is being watched. He has never learned to account for sincerity because sincerity was never useful to him.

**Delivery line (Riko, after a long pause):** "We have watched things like him before. They are very good at seeing what people want. They are very bad at understanding why someone would choose not to have it."

---

#### Tier 3: Something About the Self — Character-Specific

*Reserve these for the right player at the right moment. Do not force any of them. If the player goes somewhere true and deep, meet them there with the appropriate offering.*

**For Thornbringer GildenHeart:**
If he speaks about what completing the pilgrimage actually meant to him — not the act, but what he was afraid of finding at the end — Riko looks at him for a long moment and then says: "The tree knows your name now. Not the name your people gave you. The name the earth gives things it intends to keep." She will not explain further. But in the morning, if Thornbringer places his hand on any wood rooted in the earth, he will feel a faint pulse — not magical, not mechanical, just present. The world has noted him.

**For Yasei:**
If Yasei speaks about what spiritual sensitivity costs — the noise, the weight, the things heard that cannot be unheard — Riko goes still, and then very quietly: "There is something in the upper city that is singing in a register it should not be able to reach. We have been hearing it for thirty days. We did not know what it was. We know now that it is not the city itself." She will not say what she thinks it is. But she will look at Yasei like she is handing something delicate to the only person she trusts to hold it.

**For Aoshi:**
If Aoshi speaks about the tension between his code and what this mission requires — the places where honor and necessity have already begun to pull apart — Riko sets down her cup. "A man with a code and a man who performs a code look identical until the moment they do not. The Oni Mage cannot tell the difference. This is the only advantage you have." She pauses. "Do not waste it by being the performance."

**For Fox:**
If Fox speaks about identity — about being known, about being tracked, about what it costs to be someone who cannot afford to be fully seen — Misaki speaks instead of Riko, quietly, not for the table to hear: "There is a word in our language for what you are describing. We do not have a translation that works in yours. The closest is something like: the shape of a thing as seen by those who love it. You are afraid someone will see that shape without your permission." A pause. "The house has already seen it. We are not going to tell anyone." She slides a small paper across the table — a blank strip, like the one the sage gave them, but this one is empty. "If you ever need to walk through a space without being read — press this to your wrist. It will last until dawn."

---

#### Tier 4: Something That Changes Everything
*This tier is not assigned to a specific revelation. It is earned only if someone says something that the whole table feels — the kind of moment that silences players, not characters. If it happens, you will know. Do not reach for it. Let it find its own level.*

**If it happens, Riko closes her eyes. Her five tails go still. When she opens them:**

> "We have not heard the One Tree make a sound in two hundred years. Not since the last time something was true enough to reach it." She looks at the party, all of them, and something in her face opens in a way it has not before. "It is singing now. Very quietly. But it is singing."
>
> She refills the empty cup in the center of the table.
>
> "Whatever is happening in that city — whatever they have set in motion — the tree is paying attention. And so are we."

---

## Scene 5: The Levity Moment

**Run this during or after the exchange — whenever the energy needs relief. If the exchange has been heavy, this is the exhale. Do not skip it.**

> You become aware, at some point during the evening, that you have acquired a shadow.
>
> The young kitsune from the rafter — small, maybe equivalent to twelve or thirteen in human years, one tail and absolutely no sense of what constitutes an appropriate distance — has somehow migrated to just outside your immediate table space and is staring at your equipment with the concentrated intensity of someone who has never wanted anything more in their life.
>
> He is very obviously pretending to be invisible. He is not invisible.

**[DM note: His name is Pip. He is one-tailed, which makes him young enough that his ability to shift is unreliable. He is fascinated by the party's weapons, armor, and tools in the way children are fascinated by the things that adults use to do dangerous things. If anyone acknowledges him, he startles, looks left and right, and then points at himself with an expression that says "who, me?" — even though he is the only small fox in the immediate vicinity.]**

**Let this scene be improvised. Some starting options:**

- Pip challenges the most physically imposing party member to a drinking game with rules he makes up in real time. The rules change each round in ways that somehow always benefit him. He cannot hold his sake and shifts fully into fox form halfway through, then pretends this was intentional.
- Pip asks if he can hold someone's weapon. If he gets it, he immediately poses with it in a way that is objectively dangerous. Riko, from across the room, does not look up from her cup but says, in a completely flat voice: "Pip." He puts it down.
- Pip asks the party, in complete seriousness, if they are heroes. When told yes (or probably yes, or something in that direction), he announces that he has decided he is coming with them. Misaki, without hesitation: "You are not." Pip: "But — " Misaki: "You are absolutely not."
- If anyone gives Pip something small — a coin, a button, anything — he will have given them something back before they realize it happened. It is a rock. A smooth, perfect river rock, tucked into a pocket. It has no magical properties. He just thought they might like it.

**The goal of this scene is laughter. Do not let it run long. Two or three good beats, then let it fade naturally. Pip should end the scene on a rafter again, watching the party with open admiration, tail wagging slowly.**

---

## Scene 6: The Send-Off

*When the party signals readiness to leave — or when the pacing calls for it — let them gather themselves. Then Riko speaks one last time.*

**Do not rush this. The leave-taking is part of the scene.**

> As you rise from the table, the room does not stop — the voices continue, the cups ring, Pip has found a new audience for something — but there is a quality of attention in the space that you can feel without being able to explain. The sake house is watching you go.
>
> Riko has not moved from her cushion. She does not stand to see you out. But as you move toward the curtained door, her voice finds you across the noise, clear and unhurried:
>
> "The city above you is full of people trying to be what the moment requires. Some of them will guess right. Most will not." A pause. "You are different only in that you have been told what is true and have chosen to carry it anyway. This is rarer than it sounds."
>
> Her five tails shift, the slowest possible movement.
>
> "We will keep the lanterns lit. This is not a small thing. We have kept them unlit for a long time."

**[DM note: Let that land. Then give Misaki the final word — she is warmer, and the last voice should be warm.]**

> Misaki walks you to the door. At the curtain, she pauses, hand on the cloth.
>
> "If you come back — and I think you will come back — bring the truth of whatever happened. Not the version you tell other people. The house will know the difference."
>
> She holds the curtain aside. The lane beyond is lit amber and quiet, lanterns swaying in their sourceless breeze. At the far end, the One Tree's glow is just visible, warm and steady.
>
> "Go do the thing," she says, like it is simple, and lets the curtain fall.

## DM Running Notes

### The Economy of the House — Key Principle
The kitsune do not prize information as a commodity. They prize truth as a practice. A player who shares a real thing gets a real thing back. A player who tries to strategically disclose to extract maximum value will find Riko's tails go still and the exchange stalls. The house is not punishing them. It simply does not respond to performance. If this happens, have Misaki gently redirect: "The house is very patient. It has had a long time to learn what real sounds like."

### What Riko Looks Like When She Is Paying Attention
Her tails slow. Her expression does not change, but something behind her eyes shifts — it becomes present in a way that her default resting state, which is watchful-but-distant, is not. When all five tails go still, she has heard something that matters.

### Managing Multiple Players in the Exchange
Not every player needs to offer something. Some characters are watchers. If a player is quiet, do not force them into the economy. The house will not demand. But if you notice a player leaning in — reading the room, clearly processing — you can have Misaki turn to them specifically and ask something gentle and open-ended: "You have been listening. What is the thing you are trying to decide whether to say?"

### Time in the Sake House
The party will lose track of time here. When they leave, it will feel later than it is, or earlier, or somehow both. This is not mechanical. It is atmospheric. The kitsune village exists at a slight remove from ordinary time, and the sake house sits at its center. If players ask what time it is, Misaki will glance at the lanterns outside: "Still night. There is still night left." How much, she cannot say.

### The Paper Strip (Misaki's Gift to Fox)
This is a small, player-facing gift that rewards genuine roleplay. The strip is blank. If Fox presses it to their wrist and focuses, they can move through a space for up to one hour without being spiritually or magically read — no detection spells, no sense of their inner state. Physical identification still functions. It works once, at dawn it dissolves.

This only exists if Fox earned it through genuine roleplay. If the moment did not arrive naturally, do not force it in. It will find its time.

### Thornbringer's Gift — The Tree's Name
This is ambient and experiential, not mechanical. He does not gain a power. He gains a relationship — the world has noticed him in the specific way the natural world notices things it considers permanent. This can be expanded in later scenes at your discretion.

### What the Kitsune Will and Will Not Do Going Forward
They are not political actors. They will not send fighters with the party. They will not supply gear or resources. What they will do: remain available. If the party returns — to the village, to the area near the One Tree — the lanterns will be lit and the house will be open. What the party does with the upper city will be reported back, eventually, through channels neither the players nor the DM need to specify.

Riko's investment is real. Misaki's warmth is real. The kitsune are not a resource to be spent. They are a community that has decided, based on this evening, that these particular humans are worth watching.

### Pip
Pip can appear again whenever you need the scene to breathe. He is a reliable tonal tool. Do not overuse him. His single most important attribute: he is always watching the party from somewhere high up, and he always looks like he is trying to decide whether to follow them.

If the party ever actually lets him come with them on something, even briefly — even into the lane — give him five minutes of pure delight and then have him get distracted by something and wander off. He is fine. He always is.
