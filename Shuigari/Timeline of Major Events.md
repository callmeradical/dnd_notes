# Timeline of Major Events

## TImeline of Events of Shuigari

[h1]Timeline of Shuigari[/h1]<br />**Understanding the History of Shuigari.** The history of Shuigari is rich with war, honor, betrayal, and spiritual conflict. To the common folk, the details of ancient battles and political maneuvering are mostly unknown—passed down as stories told by elders and embellished by bards. However, the names of great Emperors, legendary Shoguns, and defining eras are well-known, often recited in the teachings of the temples or referenced in the oaths of samurai. While most people have a general understanding of the different periods and some of the major events, only the highly educated—such as scholars, nobles, and priests—know the specific details and dates. For many, the past is a source of inspiration and reverence, shaping their views of duty, honor, and destiny.<br />The Era of Sundering (Year 0 - 200 CP)<br /><br />**The Sundering.** Year 0 CP. The catastrophic event that shattered the continent of Ydrafor, dividing it into isolated islands and reshaping the world of Atellas. This era marked the end of the Jarldenholmian Empire and plunged the land into chaos as warlords fought to carve out new territoris in the aftermath of divine conflict.<br /><br />**The Rise of the Warlords.** Year 50 CP. As the surviving warlords struggled to establish control, the first great clans emerged on the island of Shugane. Rivalries and bloodshed defined this period, as each sought to dominate the fragmented archipelago.<br /><br />**The Founding of the Imperial Line.** Year 150 CP. Emperor Hosikage I, known as the “Starborn Emperor,” united the warring clans of Shugane under a spiritual banner. Legend says he was blessed by the celestial kami and descended from the heavens during the darkest days of the war. With the support of the powerful priests of the time, Hosikage established the **Imperial Line**, claiming divine right as the true ruler of Shuigari. His reign marked the beginning of the Imperial Era, though it took many more decades before the islands would truly unify.<br />[h2]The Era of Unification (Year 200 - 400 CP)[/h2]<br />**The Unification Wars.** Year 225 CP - 275 CP. Emperor Hosikage’s successors struggled to maintain unity, and the archipelago fell into a series of brutal conflicts as rival warlords challenged the Imperial line’s legitimacy. These wars finally ended when a great general, **Kazuhito Tetsu**, forged an alliance of powerful clans and crushed the rebellious daimyos.<br /><br />**Founding of the Shogunate.** Year 275 CP. Kazuhito Tetsu established the first Shogunate with the Emperor’s blessing, creating a new military regime that would govern in the Emperor’s name. This marked the beginning of the Shogunate Era, where the Shogunate wielded true political power while the Emperor remained a spiritual figurehead.<br /><br />**The Rise of the Daimyos.** Year 300 CP - 400 CP. The newly established Shogunate consolidated power by granting territories to the most powerful samurai lords, known as daimyos. This created a delicate balance between the central authority of the Shogunate and the semi-autonomous rule of the daimyos, setting the stage for centuries of political intrigue and feuding.<br />The First Shogunate (Year 400 - 600 CP)<br /><br />**The Codification of the Bushido Code.** Year 450 CP. In an effort to control the increasingly independent samurai class, the Shogunate introduced the **Bushido Code**, a strict set of rules governing the conduct of warriors. This code emphasized loyalty, honor, and obedience, becoming the foundation of samurai culture in Shugane.<br /><br />**The Great Shrine Reformation.** Year 525 CP. A period of religious conflict as rival temples vied for spiritual authority. The Shogunate intervened, standardizing temple practices and establishing the central authority of the **Temple of the Three Moons**, which became a key ally of the Shogunate.<br />[h2]The Age of Blood and Steel (Year 600 - 800 CP)[/h2]<br />**The Blood Wars.** Year 615 CP - 675 CP. A series of civil wars erupted as ambitious daimyos and even members of the Shogunate vied for control. The wars were sparked by a disputed succession, leading to decades of bloody conflict that nearly destroyed the Shogunate. Several Shoguns were assassinated, and the daimyos began to act more like independent warlords than vassals of the Shogunate.<br /><br />**The Reign of the Iron Shogun.** Year 675 CP. After years of chaos, the Iron Shogun emerged victorious, uniting the fractured land through sheer force of will. His reign marked the end of the Blood Wars and a return to order, though his methods were brutal and his rule harsh. He restored the authority of the Shogunate, but at the cost of deep-seated resentment among the daimyos.<br />[h2]The Silver Age (Year 800 - 1000 CP)[/h2]<br />**The Silver Peace.** Year 800 CP. The Silver Age began with the stabilization of the Shogunate under a series of capable, if unremarkable, Shoguns. Trade flourished, the arts were revitalized, and the capital of Hikosan grew into a center of learning and culture. During this time, the influence of the Shogunate became institutionalized, and the daimyos turned their focus to internal development.<br /><br />**The Birth of Emperor Tenno I.** Year 850 CP. Tenno I, named in honor of the Starborn Emperor, was born under auspicious signs and quickly rose to prominence as a great reformer. He is credited with initiating the **Rite of the Moons**, a sacred ritual that is still observed annually to honor the celestial kami.<br />[h2]The Waning Shogunate (Year 1000 - Present)[/h2]<br />**The Death of Emperor Tenno I.** Year 950 CP. Emperor Tenno I’s death marked the beginning of a slow decline in the spiritual authority of the Imperial Court. Without his guiding hand, the power of the Emperor began to wane, allowing the Shogunate to tighten its grip once more.<br /><br />**The Rise of the Reformist Lords.** Year 1100 CP. Discontented daimyos formed a secret alliance, the **Reformist Lords**, to oppose the Shogunate’s heavy-handed rule. They advocated for a return to a more balanced power structure, giving greater autonomy to the daimyos and reducing the Shogun’s authority.<br /><br />**The Birth of Emperor Tenno II.** Year 1170 CP. The birth of Emperor Tenno II was heralded as a return to greatness for the Imperial Line. Named after his famous ancestor, he is seen by many as a figure of hope, though he has yet to live up to the legacy of Tenno I.<br /><br />**Ascension of Shogun Mitsuhide Takara.** Year 1171 CP. Mitsuhide Takara became Shogun after the mysterious death of his predecessor, consolidating his power swiftly. Known as the “Steel Crane,” his reign has been marked by strict control and an emphasis on military strength. However, his recent decline in health has emboldened his enemies and thrown the future of the Shogunate into doubt.<br />[h2]Current Year: 1200 CP[/h2]<br />The archipelago of Shuigari is on the brink of change. With an ailing Shogun, a young Emperor, and powerful daimyos jockeying for position, the future is uncertain. The actions of a few key figures—and perhaps a handful of bold adventurers—could determine the fate of Shuigari for the next millennium.
