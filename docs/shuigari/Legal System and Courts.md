# Legal System and Courts of Shuigari

## Overview

Justice in Shuigari reflects the rigid social hierarchy and the dual authority of spiritual tradition and military power. The legal system operates on principles of collective responsibility, class-based punishments, and the absolute authority of one's lord. While the Shogunate provides overarching legal codes, implementation varies widely by prefecture, and the tension between Imperial law and Shogunate authority creates jurisdictional complexities.

## Fundamental Legal Principles

### The Four Pillars of Justice

**Loyalty (Chu 忠)**
- Obedience to one's lord is paramount
- Violating loyalty is the gravest crime
- Applies upward through hierarchy: peasant → samurai → daimyo → Shogun → Emperor

**Hierarchy (Mibun 身分)**
- Social class determines:
  - Which laws apply
  - Severity of punishment
  - Who may judge
  - Burden of proof
- A samurai and peasant accused of same crime face vastly different consequences

**Collective Responsibility (Gonin-gumi 五人組)**
- Groups of 5-10 families answerable for each other's conduct
- One family's crime brings investigation/punishment to all
- Creates powerful social pressure for conformity
- Village headman responsible for group's behavior

**Confession (Jibaku 自白)**
- Confession is "king of evidence"
- Torture permitted to obtain confession (for lower classes)
- Once confessed, punishment swift and certain
- Recanting confession after making it punished severely

### Sacred vs. Secular Law

**Imperial Law (Ritsuryō 律令)**
- Ancient legal code tied to Emperor's spiritual authority
- Covers:
  - Religious crimes (blasphemy, shrine defilement)
  - Crimes against the sacred order
  - Violations of ritual purity
  - Spiritual pollution
- Rarely invoked but carries ultimate authority
- Enforced by Imperial Guards and shrine authorities

**Shogunate Law (Buke-hō 武家法)**
- Military legal code established by Shogunate
- Governs:
  - Samurai conduct and honor
  - Daimyo obligations
  - Military crimes
  - Administrative matters
  - Inter-domain disputes
- Most commonly applied law
- Enforced by domain authorities under Shogunate oversight

**Customary Law (Kanrei 慣例)**
- Traditional village and guild practices
- Vary by region and occupation
- Govern daily disputes
- Not written but widely understood
- Lords generally respect custom unless conflicts with higher law

## Legal Code Categories

### Capital Crimes (Punishable by Death)

**Treason (Muhon 謀反)**
- Rebellion against rightful lord
- Conspiracy to overthrow Shogun or Emperor
- Aiding enemies of the realm
- Punishment: Crucifixion (for commoners), beheading (for samurai)
- Family members often executed or exiled

**Murder of Superior (Gekokujo 下克上)**
- Killing one's lord or father
- Assault on superior
- Even in self-defense, usually capital offense
- Punishment: Slow execution (crucifixion, burning)
- Family members enslaved or executed

**Arson (Kajizai 火事罪)**
- In wooden-construction society, arson threatens entire communities
- Intentional fire-setting
- Negligence causing major fire may also be capital
- Punishment: Burning (especially fitting punishment)

**Armed Robbery (Gotō 強盗)**
- Theft using weapons or violence
- Banditry
- Piracy
- Punishment: Crucifixion or beheading
- Stolen goods must be returned (family liable if goods lost)

**Counterfeiting (Gizōzai 偽造罪)**
- Creating false currency
- Forging official seals/documents
- Representing false identity to authorities
- Punishment: Crucifixion
- Family assets seized

### Major Crimes (Exile, Hard Labor, or Severe Corporal Punishment)

**Theft (Settō 窃盗)**
- Non-violent theft
- Embezzlement
- Receiving stolen goods
- Punishment varies by:
  - Value stolen (>1 ryo more serious)
  - Status of victim (stealing from lord worse)
  - Perpetrator's class (samurai face harsher judgment)
- Typical: Branding, exile, or 10-20 years hard labor

**Assault (Kegai 怪我)**
- Physical violence not resulting in death
- Severity depends on:
  - Relative class of victim/perpetrator
  - Extent of injury
  - Circumstances (provoked vs unprovoked)
- Range: Fines to exile

**Adultery (Kantsū 姦通)**
- Extramarital relations
- Both parties punished if caught
- Husband may execute adulterous wife and lover (if caught in act)
- For samurai: Loss of rank common
- For commoners: Public humiliation, fines

**Fraud (Sagi 詐欺)**
- Commercial deception
- False contracts
- Fraudulent sales
- Punishment: Fines (2-10x value defrauded), branding, exile
- Merchant guilds may impose additional sanctions

### Minor Crimes (Fines, Corporal Punishment, or Short Imprisonment)

**Gambling (Tobaku 賭博)**
- Illegal in most domains
- Punishment: Fines, beating, short imprisonment
- Enforcement inconsistent (often ignored unless excessive)

**Brawling (Kenka 喧嘩)**
- Public fighting
- Disturbing peace
- Both parties punished equally ("takes two to fight")
- Punishment: Fines, public apology, short imprisonment

**Poaching (Ryōryō 猟獲)**
- Hunting on lord's land without permission
- Taking protected animals
- Fishing in forbidden waters
- Punishment: Fines, confiscation of catch, beating

**Trespassing (Shinnyū 侵入)**
- Entering forbidden areas
- Violating curfew
- Unauthorized presence in noble districts
- Punishment: Fines, beating, short imprisonment

**Tax Evasion (Datsuzei 脱税)**
- Hiding harvest from tax collectors
- False reporting of income
- Smuggling to avoid duties
- Punishment: Pay back-taxes + fine (2-5x owed), imprisonment

## Court System

### Village/Town Courts

**Jurisdiction:**
- Minor crimes within village/town
- Civil disputes between commoners
- First level of justice for most people

**Structure:**
- Headman (Nanushi) presides
- Council of elders advises
- Public proceedings (entire village may attend)
- No formal advocates/lawyers
- Both parties speak for themselves

**Process:**
1. Complaint filed with headman
2. Investigation by headman or deputy
3. Public hearing (usually within 7 days)
4. Testimonies heard
5. Evidence presented
6. Council deliberates
7. Headman pronounces judgment
8. Punishment carried out immediately or within days

**Limitations:**
- Cannot try samurai
- Cannot impose death penalty
- Cannot try cases involving inter-village matters
- Must defer to domain courts for serious crimes

### Domain Courts

**Jurisdiction:**
- Serious crimes in domain
- Cases involving samurai
- Inter-village disputes
- Appeals from village courts
- Cases domain lord chooses to hear personally

**Structure:**
- Magistrate (Bugyō) presides
- Appointed by daimyo
- Usually samurai with legal training
- May have assistant judges
- Clerk records proceedings

**Personnel:**

*Chief Magistrate*
- Senior samurai (usually 500-2,000 koku rank)
- Oversees all legal matters in domain
- Reports directly to daimyo
- Term: Usually lifetime appointment

*District Magistrates*
- Handle cases in specific regions
- 3-10 per domain depending on size
- Rank: 200-800 koku

*Yoriki (Constables)*
- Samurai investigators
- Gather evidence
- Arrest suspects
- Interrogate witnesses
- Rank: 50-200 koku

*Doshin (Constable Assistants)*
- Lower-rank samurai or commoners
- Patrol and maintain order
- Assist investigations
- Make arrests
- Paid: 2-5 shu monthly

**Court Proceedings:**

1. **Complaint/Arrest:**
   - Formal complaint filed or suspect arrested
   - Accused brought before magistrate within 3 days

2. **Investigation (Gimmichō):**
   - Yoriki investigate facts
   - Witnesses questioned
   - Evidence gathered
   - May take days to months
   - Accused held in domain jail if flight risk

3. **Preliminary Hearing:**
   - Magistrate reviews evidence
   - Determines if case proceeds
   - May dismiss if insufficient evidence
   - May refer to village court if minor

4. **Formal Trial:**
   - Public (for minor cases) or private (serious crimes)
   - Accused kneels before magistrate
   - Charges read
   - Evidence presented
   - Witnesses testify
   - Accused may speak in defense
   - No lawyers/advocates
   
5. **Judgment:**
   - Magistrate pronounces verdict
   - Sentence issued immediately
   - Written record created
   - No appeals (except to Shogunate courts for extreme cases)

6. **Punishment:**
   - Carried out immediately or scheduled
   - Public for serious crimes
   - Private for lesser offenses

**Special Procedures:**

*Torture (Gomon)*
- Permitted for commoners only (not samurai)
- Used to obtain confession
- Methods:
  - Strappado (suspended by arms)
  - Stone pressing
  - Water torture
  - Finger crushing
- Magistrate must be present
- Confession under torture fully admissible

*Samurai Trials*
- Samurai judged by peers (samurai magistrate)
- Honor code factors heavily
- Confession expected (honorable)
- Suicide may be permitted (seppuku) rather than execution
- Family reputation considered

### Shogunate Courts

**Jurisdiction:**
- Cases involving multiple domains
- Crimes against Shogunate
- Appeals from domain courts (rare)
- Cases daimyo refers upward
- Disputes between daimyo
- Crimes by high-ranking samurai

**Structure:**
- Supreme Court (Hyōjōsho) in Hikosan
- Chief Justice (Rōjū) appointed by Shogun
- Panel of 5-7 senior judges
- All must be samurai of high rank (2,000+ koku)

**Personnel:**

*Rōjū (Elder Councillors)*
- Highest judicial authority below Shogun
- 4-5 members
- Each oversees judicial district
- May override domain court decisions

*Shogunate Magistrates (Shogun-bugyō)*
- Handle cases directly involving Shogunate interests
- Stationed in major cities
- Report to Rōjū

*Inspectors (Metsuke)*
- Investigate corruption
- Monitor daimyo compliance with law
- Secret police functions
- Report directly to Shogun

**Proceedings:**
- Similar to domain courts but more formal
- Written briefs required
- May take months or years
- Decision is final (only Shogun can overturn)

### Imperial Courts

**Jurisdiction:**
- Religious crimes
- Violations of sacred law
- Crimes involving Imperial family
- Shrine/temple disputes
- Rarely invoked

**Structure:**
- Imperial Guard of the Phoenix investigates
- Senior priests adjudicate
- Answers only to Emperor
- Operates independently of Shogunate (sometimes creates conflict)

## Criminal Investigation

### Investigation Process

**Initial Response:**
- Doshin patrol and respond to crimes
- Secure scene
- Interview witnesses
- Detain suspects if caught at scene

**Formal Investigation:**
- Yoriki assigned case
- Methods:
  - Witness interrogation
  - Physical evidence examination
  - Informant networks
  - Torture (lower classes only)
  - Divination (onmyoji may be consulted)

**Evidence Types:**
- Witness testimony (most important)
- Physical objects
- Documents
- Confession (conclusive)
- Expert testimony (rare)

**Timeline:**
- Minor crimes: 7-14 days
- Serious crimes: 30-90 days
- Complex cases: May take years

### Law Enforcement Personnel

**Domain Guard/Police:**
- Maintain order in castle towns
- Patrol and presence
- Respond to crimes
- Numbers: 50-500 depending on city size

**Magistrate's Office:**
- Yoriki: 10-30 per domain
- Doshin: 50-300 per domain
- Clerical staff: 20-100

**Special Investigators:**
- Onmyoji (supernatural crimes)
- Monk investigators (temple crimes)
- Merchant guild enforcers (commercial crimes)

### Bounty Hunters

**Okappiki (Unofficial Constables)**
- Commoners working for magistrates
- Paid per arrest or by case
- Know criminal underworld
- Questionable methods tolerated
- Payment: 500 mon - 5 ryo per capture

**Rewards for Information:**
- Minor criminals: 100-500 mon
- Major criminals: 1-10 ryo
- Fugitives from justice: 5-50 ryo

## Civil Law

### Property Disputes

**Land Ownership:**
- All land ultimately belongs to lord
- Peasants have use rights, not ownership
- Samurai granted fiefs (can be revoked)
- Merchants may own property in cities

**Dispute Resolution:**
1. Parties attempt negotiation
2. Village headman mediates
3. If unresolved, appeal to magistrate
4. Magistrate investigates ownership records
5. Decision based on:
   - Written deeds
   - Tax records
   - Witness testimony
   - Traditional usage
6. Decision final

**Inheritance:**
- Primogeniture (eldest son inherits)
- Lord may approve alternate heir
- Daughters inherit if no sons (with restrictions)
- Property cannot be divided (maintains family holdings)

### Contract Law

**Binding Agreements:**
- Written contracts recognized
- Witnesses required (usually 2-3)
- Sealed with personal seal (hanko)
- Registered with magistrate for major contracts

**Breach of Contract:**
- Injured party files complaint
- Magistrate reviews contract
- Evidence of breach examined
- Remedies:
  - Specific performance ordered
  - Monetary damages (rare)
  - Cancellation of contract
  - Punishment for fraudulent breach

**Common Contracts:**
- Land leases
- Merchant agreements
- Loans and debts
- Marriage agreements
- Apprenticeship terms
- Service contracts

### Debt Collection

**Legal Debt:**
- Creditor may petition magistrate
- Debtor summoned to court
- If debt proven:
  - Payment ordered
  - Property may be seized
  - Labor service may be required
  - Imprisonment possible (but limits earning ability)

**Illegal Collection:**
- Violence prohibited
- Harassment beyond reasonable limits illegal
- Creditors may not seize sacred items
- Limits on seizing tools needed for livelihood

**Bankruptcy:**
- No formal bankruptcy law
- Debtors may flee to temples (sanctuary)
- Family members liable for debts
- Some debts forgiven after 20 years (custom, not law)

## Punishments

### Execution Methods

**Beheading (Zanzai)**
- Standard execution for samurai
- Quick and considered honorable
- Performed by skilled executioner
- Body returned to family

**Crucifixion (Haritsukezai)**
- Common for serious crimes
- Public display
- Death takes hours/days
- Body left on display as warning
- Reserved for commoners

**Burning (Yakibachizai)**
- Arson and religious crimes
- Public spectacle
- Extremely painful
- Rare but memorable

**Seppuku (Ritual Suicide)**
- Permitted for samurai
- Alternative to execution
- Restores family honor
- Performed with ceremony
- Assisted by second (kaishakunin) who beheads

### Corporal Punishments

**Tattooing (Irezumi)**
- Permanent mark of criminality
- Location indicates crime:
  - Forehead: Murder attempt
  - Arm: Theft
  - Back: Fraud
- Social stigma
- Cannot be removed

**Beating (Takezai)**
- Bamboo cane on back
- 50-100 strikes typical
- Public or private
- Heals but painful

**Amputation (Danzetsu)**
- Fingers for theft
- Hand for repeated theft
- Prevents future crime
- Marks criminal permanently

**Branding (Yakibanagezai)**
- Hot iron mark
- Forehead or face
- Permanent identification
- Social outcast status

### Non-Corporal Punishments

**Exile (Ruienzai)**
- Banishment from domain
- May be temporary (5-20 years) or permanent
- Must leave within days
- Return means death penalty
- Family may be exiled too

**House Arrest (Chikkyo)**
- Confined to home
- Duration: Months to years
- Samurai punishment (avoids dishonor)
- Monitored by guards

**Imprisonment (Rōya)**
- Domain jail
- Conditions poor
- Used for:
  - Awaiting trial
  - Short sentences (days to months)
  - Debt imprisonment
- Fees charged for food/bedding

**Fines (Kasanzai)**
- Monetary penalty
- Amount varies by crime and wealth
- Failure to pay → labor or imprisonment
- Family liable if individual cannot pay

**Public Apology (Chinsha)**
- Formal apology ceremony
- Loss of face
- Milder punishment
- Often combined with fine

## Samurai Privileges

### Legal Immunities

**Kirisute Gomen (Permission to Cut Down)**
- Samurai may kill commoner who insults them
- Must report to magistrate
- Justification examined
- Abuse punished, but standard is lenient
- Creates fear and respect for samurai class

**Trial by Peers:**
- Samurai judged by samurai
- Higher standard of evidence
- Torture prohibited
- May demand trial by combat (rare, must be approved)

**Milder Punishments:**
- Where commoner faces crucifixion, samurai may receive seppuku
- Where commoner receives branding, samurai receives exile
- Honor-based punishments preferred

### Samurai Crimes

**Daimei (Dishonor to One's Name)**
- Conduct unbecoming a samurai
- Cowardice in battle
- Financial misconduct
- Drunkenness and brawling
- Punishment: Loss of rank, reduced stipend, confinement

**Failure of Duty:**
- Falling asleep on guard
- Losing lord's property
- Administrative incompetence
- May result in seppuku being "suggested"

## Temple Law and Sanctuary

### Sanctuary Rights

**Temple Asylum:**
- Criminals may seek refuge in major temples
- Tradition of sanctuary respected (usually)
- Limits:
  - Treason: No sanctuary
  - Murder of samurai: No sanctuary
  - Crimes against temples: No sanctuary
- May remain for months or years
- Eventually must emerge or take vows

**Monastic Law:**
- Temples govern their own members
- Buddhist/Shinto authorities judge religious crimes
- Domain courts rarely interfere
- Exceptions: Serious crimes affecting laypeople

## Current Legal Tensions

### Jurisdictional Conflicts

**Shogunate vs. Imperial:**
- Some crimes fall under both codes
- Which takes precedence?
- Usually Shogunate wins but risks alienating traditionalists

**Domain Autonomy:**
- Daimyo resist Shogunate interference
- "Internal domain matter" claimed often
- Shogunate forcing centralization
- Leads to passive resistance

### Reform Movements

**Merchant Class Justice:**
- Merchants demanding equal legal standing
- Guild courts handling commercial disputes
- Some daimyo accepting this
- Traditionalists resist

**Peasant Rights:**
- Some domains experimenting with formal peasant courts
- Appeals process from village headman
- Reduces arbitrary punishment
- Controversial innovation

### Corruption

**Bribery:**
- Magistrates may be bribed
- Wealthy can buy favorable judgments
- Open secret in many domains
- Shogunate cracks down periodically

**False Accusations:**
- Enemies may frame rivals
- Planted evidence
- Coerced testimony
- Magistrates supposed to uncover but don't always

## Campaign Integration

### Plot Hooks
- PC framed for crime they didn't commit
- Corrupt magistrate must be exposed
- Fugitive seeks sanctuary, pursuers don't respect it
- Domain court vs Shogunate court jurisdictional battle
- Obsidian Circle has infiltrated magistrate's office

### Legal Complications
- PC kills in self-defense but victim was superior
- Evidence of crime but getting it requires illegal methods
- Witness won't testify (fear, bribery, loyalty)
- Magistrate demands confession but PC innocent

### Using the System
- PCs can file formal complaints
- Investigations take time (create urgency)
- Social class affects outcomes
- Lawyers don't exist but influential friends help
- Bribes work (but carry risks)

### Punishment as Story
- Exile creates wandering PC
- Tattooing marks PC visually
- House arrest creates time pressure
- Trial scene as dramatic set piece
- Seppuku as character exit
